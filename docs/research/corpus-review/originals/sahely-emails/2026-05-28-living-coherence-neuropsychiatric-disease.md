## Executive summary (verbatim from Gmail subscription email)

A deep dive into mental illness, psychiatry, embodiment, and the life-coherent processes that shape suffering, diagnosis, and healing.

This episode explores a central question:

What if mental illness is not simply a broken brain, a chemical imbalance, or a diagnostic label — but a disturbance in the process of living itself?

The conversation begins with a contrast between a visible fracture and an invisible panic attack. In one case, the X-ray makes the diagnosis obvious. In the other, the suffering is immense, but the usual medical tools struggle to make it visible. From there, the episode opens into a wider inquiry into the limits of modern psychiatry and the need for a more integrated, life-coherent framework.

Drawing on autopoiesis, structural coupling, emotioning, interoception, neurobiology, phenomenology, public health, and transdiagnostic psychiatry, this deep dive asks how distress emerges when a living organism can no longer maintain viable coupling with its world.

The episode does not reject existing psychiatric diagnoses. Instead, it asks what lies beneath them. A diagnosis may describe the surface pattern of collapse, but it may not explain the life process by which that collapse occurred. Depression, anxiety, trauma, addiction, psychosis, burnout, autism, ADHD, and other forms of suffering are explored as disturbances in living coherence: disruptions in constraint, margin, state, disturbance, perception, regulation, and options.
