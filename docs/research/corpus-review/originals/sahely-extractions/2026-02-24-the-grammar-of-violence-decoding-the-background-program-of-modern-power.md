## Verbatim key claims (16 claims; high-fidelity)

### C-1 (p6, Abstract) — Structural-grammar reframe of crises
> "Modern crises — military escalation, ecological destabilization, financial volatility, widening inequality, and institutional erosion — are commonly treated as discrete failures. This work argues that such events are **systemic outputs of an underlying structural grammar** that shapes incentives, moral narratives, and institutional design."

### C-2 (p6, Abstract) — Three interlocking mechanisms (LOAD-BEARING)
> "Drawing on peace research (the violence triangle), systems theory, political economy, and ecological economics, the book identifies **three interlocking mechanisms**: (1) cultural legitimation of structural harm, (2) institutional reinforcement of extractive growth, and (3) recursive feedback loops that convert crisis into confirmation of prevailing assumptions."

### C-3 (p6, Abstract) — Distinguishing structural critique from conspiracy
> "Distinguishing structural critique from conspiracy thinking, the work proposes a redesign grounded in viability-first principles. It advances a constraint-based framework in which life-support systems — ecological stability, public health, social cohesion, and institutional trust — become primary evaluative standards. The goal is not moral indictment but structural clarity."

### C-4 (p6, Exec Summary §1 Core Claim) — Self-reinforcing structural architecture
> "Recurring global crises are not isolated accidents. They are predictable outputs of a self-reinforcing structural architecture composed of cultural narratives, institutional incentives, and feedback loops. This architecture privileges self-maximization, perpetual growth, and security through dominance."

### C-5 (p7, Exec Summary §2 Structural Model) — Three foundational layers (LOAD-BEARING)
> "The analysis integrates three foundational layers:
> • **The Violence Triangle**: Cultural narratives legitimize structural arrangements that generate direct harm. Harm, in turn, reinforces those narratives.
> • **The Dualistic Archetype (DMA)**: Binary 'good versus evil' framing escalates conflict and inhibits structural introspection.
> • **The Ruling Value Code**: Rationality is equated with self-maximization, producing extractive growth dynamics that destabilize ecological and social systems.
> These layers form a closed loop in which crisis validates the assumptions that produced it."

### C-6 (p7, Exec Summary §3 War State as Output) — Militarization as structural response
> "Militarization, projection, and just war doctrine are examined not as isolated policies but as structural responses to internal instability. The security paradigm equating safety with force accumulation intensifies systemic risk under conditions of global interdependence."

### C-7 (p7, Exec Summary §4 Cancer Logic) — Cancer-logic at societal scale (LOAD-BEARING; composes with Wave-3 #2 Metastasis to Meta-stasis)
> "When growth detaches from regenerative constraint, systems exhibit pathology analogous to unregulated proliferation. Capital accumulation without life-function accountability leads to ecological overshoot, financial fragility, and inequality concentration."

### C-8 (p7, Exec Summary §5 Breaking the Loop) — Structural-not-conspiratorial persistence
> "Structural persistence arises from incentive alignment, institutional lock-in, and psychological comfort in moral certainty — **not necessarily from centralized conspiracy**. Effective redesign requires altering incentive architecture rather than targeting individuals alone."

### C-9 (p7, Exec Summary §6 Viability as First Principle) — Constraint-first governance (LOAD-BEARING)
> "The proposed alternative reframes rationality around system viability. **Life-support thresholds — ecological integrity, public health, institutional stability, and social cohesion — become non-negotiable design constraints.** Constraint-first governance embeds feedback mechanisms that prevent systems from legitimizing their own destruction."

### C-10 (p7, Exec Summary §7 Civilizational Transition) — Holistic-Dialectical-Transcendent (HDT) alternative
> "A shift from dualistic escalation to **holistic–dialectical–transcendent processing of conflict** reduces annihilative dynamics. Security becomes resilience; growth becomes regeneration; power becomes system coherence."

### C-11 (p7-8, Exec Summary Conclusion) — Code-revision-before-collapse-correction
> "The work advances a structural thesis: when the underlying grammar of modern power is made visible, participation becomes conscious. Conscious systems can redesign themselves. The question is not whether crises will continue under the current code, but whether the code will be revised before correction is imposed by collapse."

### C-12 (p9, §1.1 The Feeling That Something Is Off) — Pattern-recurrence claim
> "Wars recur. Markets crash. Ecosystems destabilize. Public institutions hollow out. Trust erodes. And yet each crisis is presented as discrete... The unease emerges not from any single failure, but from recurrence. The crises are different in form, but similar in pattern: **short-term gain, long-term destabilization; concentration of benefit, diffusion of harm; moral rhetoric, material extraction**."

### C-13 (p10, §1.2 From Events to Structure) — Structural-thinking pivot
> "Event-thinking asks: Who did this? When did it happen? How did it go wrong? Structural thinking asks: What incentives were in place? What values were encoded? What outcomes are repeatedly selected for? When crises recur under different leadership, in different countries, across decades, a personal explanation weakens. When distinct sectors produce analogous distortions — short-term extraction over long-term stability — coincidence becomes improbable."

### C-14 (p10, §1.2 closing) — Structure ≠ conspiracy (LOAD-BEARING)
> "Structure is not a conspiracy. It is a set of rules, incentives, and value hierarchies that generate predictable outputs... No secret meeting required. The rules are sufficient."

### C-15 (p11, §1.3 What It Means to See the Code) — Background-code metaphor (LOAD-BEARING — parallels Spore canon-as-substrate-grammar self-understanding)
> "In computational systems, code defines permitted operations, default priorities, and optimization targets. Once written, it runs silently. It produces outputs that appear autonomous. Social systems also contain defaults. What counts as rational? What counts as success? What counts as security? What counts as progress? ... These definitions function as **background code**. They are rarely debated explicitly. They are assumed."

### C-16 (p11, §1.3 closing) — Three levels for seeing the code
> "To 'see the code' is to identify these embedded assumptions. It requires distinguishing three levels:
> • **Direct outcomes** — visible harm.
> • **Structural arrangements** — incentive systems.
> • **Cultural narratives** — value justifications."
