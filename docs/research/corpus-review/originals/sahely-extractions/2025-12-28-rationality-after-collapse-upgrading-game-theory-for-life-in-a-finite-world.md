## Verbatim key claims

### C-1 (p3, Abstract) — Diagnosis (LOAD-BEARING)
> "Modern societies rely on formal models of rational choice to guide decisions in economics, governance, public health, and technology. Chief among these is game theory, a framework widely regarded as analytically rigorous and value-neutral. Yet across domains — from pandemic preparedness to climate governance — decisions deemed 'rational' within these models have produced outcomes that undermine the conditions required for human and planetary life to continue and flourish."

### C-2 (p3, Abstract) — Axioms-not-misapplication thesis
> "This white paper argues that the problem lies not in misapplication or moral failure, but in the **axioms of rationality embedded in dominant decision models themselves**. By auditing the hidden assumptions of game theory, the paper shows that it is structurally blind to life necessities, commons, prevention, and long-term viability. As a result, it cannot detect the conditions of its own failure."

### C-3 (p3, Abstract) — Life-range-expansion as new rationality definition
> "Drawing on John McMurtry's Life-Value Onto-Axiology, the paper proposes a constructive upgrade: redefining rationality in terms of **life-range expansion** — the preservation and growth of the coherent capacities for thought, felt being, and action across time. It replaces equilibrium with viability as the primary success criterion and introduces universal life necessities as non-negotiable constraints on rational choice."

### C-4 (p3, Abstract) — Central claim (LOAD-BEARING)
> "Its central claim is practical and urgent: **rational systems that cannot see life cannot sustain it — and therefore cannot sustain themselves**."

### C-5 (p4, Executive Summary) — Game-theory-life-blind-by-construction (LOAD-BEARING for Spore F6 failure-modes ADR-0075 + ADR-0048 substitution-trap)
> "The paper's core diagnosis is that dominant models of rational choice — especially game theory — are **life-blind by construction**. They assume self-maximizing agents, fixed preferences, and payoff optimization within given rules, while excluding from consideration the life-support conditions that make rational agency possible in the first place. Universal life necessities, commons, prevention, and long-term viability are treated as externalities rather than governing constraints."

### C-6 (p4, Exec Summary) — Cannot-recognize-own-failure (LOAD-BEARING for F6.8 meta-pattern/composition failure category)
> "As a result, these models cannot recognize when 'rational' strategies cumulatively destroy the conditions of life. Collapse appears as an external shock rather than a predictable outcome. This structural blindness explains repeated failures in pandemic preparedness, climate mitigation, financial stability, and environmental protection."

### C-7 (p4, Exec Summary) — Four-axiom upgrade
> "Within this framework: Value is defined as that which enables a more coherently inclusive range of thought, felt being, and action. Universal life necessities function as non-negotiable constraints, not tradeable preferences. Rational action is redefined as that which preserves and expands future life-range. **Equilibrium is replaced by viability as the primary criterion of success.**"

### C-8 (p4, Exec Summary) — COVID→climate→AI continuum (LOAD-BEARING context-anchor)
> "The paper situates this framework across the COVID → climate → AI continuum, arguing that as decision systems become faster, more automated, and more abstract, the cost of life-blind rationality grows exponentially. Without explicit life-coherent constraints, advanced analytics and artificial intelligence risk accelerating civilizational failure rather than preventing it."

### C-9 (p4, Exec Summary) — Operational conclusion
> "Rationality must now be constrained by life viability if it is to remain rational at all."

### C-10 (p5, §1 Introduction) — Axiomatic-level diagnosis
> "The central claim of this paper is that the problem lies one level deeper than policy failure or moral weakness. It lies in the **dominant model of rationality itself**. What modern societies call 'rational choice' is governed by assumptions that systematically exclude life requirements from consideration. As a result, decision systems can operate flawlessly according to their internal logic while simultaneously eroding the very foundations that make rational choice possible."

### C-11 (p14, §10 Life-Value Coherent Game Model) — Governing-frame-not-tools shift
> "Upgrading game theory does not require abandoning its analytical strengths. Strategic interaction, incentive sensitivity, and formal clarity remain valuable. What changes is the **governing frame** within which these tools operate."

### C-12 (p14, §10) — Life-support conditions as explicit model state (LOAD-BEARING for Spore F1 sensor-oracle-governance)
> "A life-value-coherent model begins by redefining the game space itself. Instead of assuming a fixed arena with given rules and payoffs, the model explicitly represents the **life-support conditions that make the game possible**. These conditions — public health capacity, ecological regeneration rates, social trust, knowledge commons — are not background constants. They are dynamic stocks of life capital that can grow, degrade, or collapse depending on collective behavior."

### C-13 (p14, §10) — Viability thresholds as invalid-strategy filters (LOAD-BEARING for Spore F5 actuator-logic R-doctrine)
> "Agents still pursue goals, but their strategy sets are constrained by viability thresholds. Strategies that undermine essential life capacities are excluded as invalid, just as strategies that violate physical constraints are excluded in engineering models. This is not moral censorship; it is **rational boundary setting**."

### C-14 (p14, §10) — Cooperation as rational default
> "Importantly, cooperation is no longer treated as an anomaly requiring special explanation. When life conditions are explicit, cooperative behavior often emerges as the rational default, because the preservation of shared life capital directly benefits all agents' future agency. Institutions, regulations, and commons governance appear not as distortions of rationality, but as **rational infrastructure**."

### C-15 (p14, §10) — Asymmetric power-responsibility (LOAD-BEARING for Spore ADR-0047 Layer 2 + ADR-0050 joint-commitment)
> "Such a model also incorporates asymmetry and power explicitly. Actors with disproportionate capacity to damage life systems are subject to stricter constraints, reflecting the reality that **unequal power entails unequal responsibility**. Rationality is thus re-anchored in real-world conditions rather than abstract symmetry."

### C-16 (p14, §10) — New metric: keeping-the-game-alive (HIGHEST-LEVERAGE FRAMING SHIFT)
> "In this framework, strategic intelligence is measured not by winning the game, but by **keeping the game alive**."

### C-17 (p15, §11 Why This Upgrade Is No Longer Optional) — Failure-of-axioms-not-intelligence
> "This is not a failure of intelligence. It is a failure of axioms."

### C-18 (p15, §11) — Conservative-in-deepest-sense reframe
> "In this sense, the proposal is neither utopian nor radical. It is conservative in the deepest meaning of the word: it seeks to conserve the conditions of life. The choice is not between markets and planning, competition and cooperation, freedom and constraint. The choice is between **a rationality that cannot see life and one that cannot ignore it**. Only the latter can guide a viable future."

### C-19 (p16, Afterword) — AI-systematic-danger-from-life-blind-game (LOAD-BEARING — composes with Spore agent-stewardship arc)
> "Artificial intelligence brings the problem into sharp relief. AI systems increasingly operate at speeds, scales, and levels of abstraction beyond direct human oversight. If they are trained on, or embedded within, life-blind rationality frameworks — optimizing proxy metrics, efficiency scores, or payoff functions divorced from life conditions — they will faithfully accelerate the very patterns that are already failing. An AI that optimizes within a life-blind game does not become neutral or objective; it becomes **systematically dangerous**, precisely because it cannot recognize the conditions of its own failure."

### C-20 (p16, Afterword) — Closing line
> "The task ahead is not to abandon reason, but to finish it."
