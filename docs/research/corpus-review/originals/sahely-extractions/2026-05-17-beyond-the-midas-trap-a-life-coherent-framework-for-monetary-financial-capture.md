## 9-fact anchor-hand-curated extraction

1. **Midas Trap central thesis** [pdf-p3-5 Abstract + Executive Summary] — verbatim: "The Midas Trap is the condition in which civilization protects financial claims more reliably than the living conditions from which all real value arises." Reframes the ancient Midas myth as civilizational diagnostic: not greed-of-individuals but systemic-conversion-of-living-realities-into-monetizable-claims. **Land becomes real estate. Housing becomes asset class. Education becomes debt-financed credentialing. Health becomes billing stream. Care becomes unpaid background labor or commodified service. Nature becomes resource/offset/natural capital/collateral. Attention becomes data. War becomes security market. Public goods become investment opportunities. Future generations become implicit debtors of present extraction.** (verbatim list, pdf-p3).

2. **Bank credit creation as load-bearing premise** [pdf-p2 Abstract] — verbatim: "The Bank of England's own account confirms a key premise: in modern economies, most money is created by commercial banks when they make loans, and banks do not simply lend out pre-existing deposits in the textbook intermediary model (McLeay et al., 2014; Jakab & Kumhof, 2015)." Sahely engages **Werner's bank-credit-creation theory** as empirical (not heterodox-speculative) substrate. Bank of England official paper as legitimating institutional citation. First Sahely paper explicitly engaging modern-monetary mechanics.

3. **10-author substrate-integration** [pdf-p2 Abstract] — explicit substrate-citation list: "**McMurtry**'s life-value onto-axiology and diagnosis of money-value sequencing; **Hudson**'s analysis of rentier finance and neo-feudal extraction; **Werner**'s theory of bank credit creation and credit allocation; **Keen**'s account of private-debt instability; **Lietaer**'s monetary-diversity and monetary-monoculture framework; **Modern Monetary Theory**'s critique of fiscal myths and false household analogies; **Mosley**'s democratic challenge to bank-created money; **Galtung**'s structural violence; **Ostrom**'s commons governance; and **Wilber**'s developmental warning concerning technically advanced but morally immature institutions." NEW substrate attributions: Hudson + Werner + Keen + Lietaer + MMT + Mosley + Wilber. **First Sahely paper to engage heterodox-monetary-theory cluster at full doctrinal depth.**

4. **Claim-sovereignty as the underlying disorder** [pdf-p3 Executive Summary] — verbatim: "The paper does not argue that money, finance, markets, credit, property, or corporations are inherently evil. These are powerful social instruments... The problem arises when these instruments become **self-protecting abstractions**. At that point, the living world is no longer the criterion. Financial claims become the criterion. Debt must be serviced. Asset values must be protected. Investor confidence must be preserved..." Method-precedent: distinguish-instrument-from-absolutization. The corrective is not anti-money but **restoration of money/credit/property/law/technology/governance to life-service**.

5. **Decent-people-participating-in-harmful-outcomes pattern** [pdf-p3 Executive Summary] — verbatim: "Modern institutions often make decent people participate in harmful outcomes while preserving their self-image. A banker may believe he is allocating capital rationally. A central banker may believe she is protecting stability. A government may believe it is maintaining credibility. A corporation may believe it is fulfilling fiduciary duty. A household may believe it is simply trying to survive. A pension fund may believe it is protecting retirees. Yet the total system produces..." (Abstract continues). Generalizes ADR-0048 substitution-trap to multi-actor distributed-agency at financial-system layer. **Banality-of-financial-harm** as systemic failure-mode.

6. **No legitimate claim if enforcement requires disposability of life** [pdf-p2 Abstract closing] — verbatim: "The central claim is that humanity will not escape the Midas Trap by better growth, smarter finance, greener investment, technological innovation, or philanthropic compensation alone. It must restore money, credit, property, law, technology, and governance to life-service. The highest realism is no longer financial growth, but **viability**. **No financial claim is legitimate if its enforcement requires the disposability of life.**" Final-principle statement. Echoes Wave-12 anchor #6 §15 closing ("Let the economy... justify itself before life") but adds operational-legitimacy-test: enforcement-cost in life-disposability.

7. **Life-ground vs life-capacity vs claim-sovereignty triad** [Keywords + Abstract] — three-term operational architecture: **life-ground** (the living substrate from which all real value arises) / **life-capacity** (the protected/repaired/expanded organism-niche viability) / **claim-sovereignty** (the failure-mode when financial claims become sovereign over life-ground). Method-precedent: distinguish-substrate-from-product-from-pathology at monetary layer.

8. **Public banking + credit allocation + monetary democracy** [Keywords] — three operational levers named explicitly: public banking (credit-creation in life-service); credit allocation (selective-channeling of bank-created money toward life-coherent ends, NOT financial speculation); monetary democracy (democratic challenge to bank-created money authority). Adds operational-architecture to Wave-12 anchor #6 §15 (which named the verbs without specifying institutional-mechanisms).

9. **De-implementation + organized-not-knowing** [Keywords] — two failure-mode names: **de-implementation** (the operational verb required when current institutions actively reproduce life-incoherence; must be UN-built, not just balanced) + **organized not-knowing** (collective epistemic-capture; people inside institutions trained NOT to see the system's life-incoherence). Both generalize ADR-0075 F6 failure-modes to monetary-financial layer. De-implementation appears in Wave-12 anchor #6 §8.1 11-verb stack; here it gets specific monetary-financial operationalization.
