## Verbatim key claims (12 claims; high-fidelity for Spore viability bridge work)

### C-1 (p7, Abstract) — Viability illiteracy thesis (LOAD-BEARING)
> "This white paper proposes that a central driver of today's metacrisis is **viability illiteracy** — a widespread inability to recognize, name, and protect the life-conditions upon which all social, economic, and institutional systems depend."

### C-2 (p7, Abstract) — Reframe from moral decline / tech insufficiency to literacy
> "Rather than attributing current failures to moral decline, technological insufficiency, or ideological conflict, the paper reframes the crisis as a loss of orientation: signals have drifted away from the realities they are meant to represent."

### C-3 (p7, Abstract) — Universal grammar of viability framing (LOAD-BEARING for Spore viability-grammar substrate)
> "Drawing on health, economics, ecology, and lived human experience, the paper introduces a **universal grammar of viability**: a simple, humane framework that reconnects life-requirements, life-support systems, and the measurements that guide decision-making. Emphasis is placed on coherence, capacity, continuity, and care, rather than speed, scale, or abstract growth."

### C-4 (p8, Executive Summary) — Life-ground definition (LOAD-BEARING; recurs across the cluster)
> "The paper introduces the concept of the **life-ground**: the universal conditions every human life requires — including clean air and water, nutritious food, care, learning, safety, time for rest and connection, and a living environment. These necessities are not ideological preferences or commodities; they are preconditions for any viable society."

### C-5 (p8, Executive Summary) — Three-question diagnostic protocol (LOAD-BEARING — cluster-signature)
> "Building on this foundation, the paper outlines a universal grammar of viability, organized around three simple questions that can be applied at any scale:
> 1. What does life actually require here to function and flourish?
> 2. What systems are meant to provide those conditions?
> 3. What are we measuring or rewarding instead?"

### C-6 (p8, Executive Summary) — Repair-as-ongoing-re-anchoring (NOT total overhaul)
> "By repeatedly asking these questions, individuals and institutions can reconnect signals to reality, reduce fragmentation, and restore coherence without requiring consensus or centralized control. Repair is framed not as total overhaul, but as ongoing re-anchoring — protecting capacity, preserving margins, and preventing unnecessary harm."

### C-7 (p8, Executive Summary) — Fidelity-not-domination measure of success
> "The paper concludes by emphasizing that it may indeed be too late for painless transitions or guaranteed outcomes. However, it is not too late to reduce suffering, prevent cascading failures, transmit clarity to future generations, and maintain dignity and care under constraint. The ultimate measure of success is not domination of outcomes, but fidelity to life-conditions in an uncertain world."

### C-8 (p10, §0.2 Why This Paper Exists) — Literacy problem, not moral problem
> "This gap is not primarily moral. It is not the result of bad intentions or collective failure of character. It is a **literacy problem**. Just as financial illiteracy can lead people to make decisions that quietly undermine their security, viability illiteracy can lead societies to optimize for signals that slowly erode the foundations they depend on."

### C-9 (p10, §0.2 Why This Paper Exists) — Three modest-and-serious purposes
> "Its purpose is modest and serious at the same time:
> • to restore attention to the basic conditions that allow human life to function and flourish,
> • to help distinguish between symbols and realities,
> • and to offer a common set of questions that can be asked in homes, workplaces, institutions, and communities alike."

### C-10 (p11, §1.1 Why This Feeling Is Hard to Name) — Systemic-misalignment-felt-at-human-scale (LOAD-BEARING for Spore's Membrane-as-sensing primitive)
> "What people are sensing is not merely personal strain. It is **systemic misalignment felt at a human scale**. We are exquisitely sensitive beings, evolved to notice when the conditions that support life are becoming unstable. Long before collapse becomes visible in headlines or data, it is felt as:
> • tension without a clear source,
> • fatigue that rest alone does not resolve,
> • difficulty trusting long-term promises,
> • a subtle grief for futures imagined but no longer assumed."

### C-11 (p12, §1.2 When Feeling Is Ahead of Language) — Felt-dissonance as early recognition
> "Sometimes what we are feeling is not confusion — it is **early recognition**. Recognition of:
> • systems moving faster than the conditions they depend on,
> • decisions optimized for short horizons colliding with long-lived consequences,
> • signals of success drifting away from lived well-being.
> These perceptions do not arrive as neat conclusions. They arrive as **felt dissonance**."

### C-12 (p12, §1.3 The Cost of Dismissing This Feeling) — Diagnostic-not-pathologizing stance
> "When this quiet feeling is dismissed or pathologized, several things tend to happen: People numb themselves through distraction or overwork. Others harden into certainty, ideology, or outrage. Some retreat into private concern without shared action. None of these responses are moral failures. They are coping strategies in the absence of shared meaning. But they come at a cost."

| **Measurements / projections** | The signals (metrics, models, indicators) used to guide decisions | "What are we measuring or rewarding instead?" |

| **Coherence** | Healing direction: signals re-anchored to lived realities | The goal of re-literacy |
