## Verbatim key claims (15 claims; selective-high-fidelity)

### C-1 (p7, Abstract — LOAD-BEARING) — Self-reframing as dynamic extension
> "Why do civilizations collapse, and under what conditions can they repair themselves before systemic breakdown occurs? This paper develops an integrated framework for analyzing civilizational stability by examining the dynamic interactions among **ecological systems, institutional governance, cultural narratives, political power, and information environments**. Building upon the original violence–viability architecture, the analysis expands the model to incorporate **biological stress transmission, political economy constraints, temporal lag dynamics, and historical pathways of institutional transformation**."

### C-2 (p7, Abstract) — Viability corridor + cascading fragility (recurrent)
> "The framework proposes that societies remain stable within a 'viability corridor' when **life-ground integrity, institutional capacity, and cultural orientation remain mutually reinforcing**. When these domains become misaligned — through ecological degradation, institutional capture, or polarized narratives — **cascading fragility** may emerge, increasing the likelihood of systemic conflict and collapse."

### C-3 (p7, Abstract — LOAD-BEARING) — Repair-pathway claim
> "However, historical evidence demonstrates that societies can occasionally interrupt these trajectories through **institutional redesign, expansion of civil commons institutions, and new forms of cooperative governance**. By synthesizing insights from peace research, ecological economics, complexity theory, neuroscience, and political economy, the violence–viability framework offers both a conceptual map and a practical diagnostic tool for assessing civilizational resilience in an era of intensifying ecological and geopolitical pressures."

### C-4 (p15, §1.3 — LOAD-BEARING; DISTINCTIVE; SELF-CRITIQUE) — Missing transmission mechanisms
> "The original framework proposed that systemic misalignment across ecological, institutional, and cultural layers can trigger population-level shifts toward threat perception and violence. However, **it did not specify the mechanisms through which millions of individual nervous systems become synchronized in shared emotional and cognitive states**. Without identifying the pathways that link individual neurobiology to macro-social dynamics, the concept of a 'collective nervous system' risks remaining metaphorical rather than operational."

### C-5 (p16, §1.3 — SELF-CRITIQUE limitation 2) — Insufficient HDT empirical anchoring
> "The original paper documented numerous examples of civilizational collapse or systemic breakdown. However, the proposed transition toward a more reflexive, cooperative civilizational orientation — described through Galtung's HDT (holism–dialectics–transcendence) — was **less empirically grounded**... Without such empirical anchors, the model risks appearing normatively aspirational rather than historically grounded."

### C-6 (p16, §1.3 — SELF-CRITIQUE limitation 3 — LOAD-BEARING) — Power-asymmetry blind-spot
> "The policy diagnostic tools proposed in the initial framework implicitly assumed that societies will attempt to resolve problems when they possess the physical capacity to do so. **History repeatedly demonstrates that this assumption is often false.** Entrenched economic interests, regulatory capture, geopolitical competition, and elite power structures frequently block adaptive responses — even when solutions are technologically and economically feasible. **Any realistic framework of civilizational viability must therefore incorporate the political economy of intentional obstruction and vested interests.**"

### C-7 (p16-17, §1.3 — SELF-CRITIQUE limitation 4 — LOAD-BEARING; DISTINCTIVE) — Temporal elasticity gap
> "The original multiplicative viability function suggests that collapse occurs immediately when one component approaches failure. **Real societies behave very differently.** Complex systems often maintain the appearance of stability while internally degrading. Institutions borrow from the future through debt. Ecological reserves are depleted faster than they regenerate. Cultural narratives maintain legitimacy long after material conditions deteriorate. These processes create **temporal elasticity**, allowing systems to stretch beyond their sustainable limits for extended periods before reaching critical thresholds."

### C-8 (p17, §1.4 — LOAD-BEARING) — Four extension directions (META-METHOD)
> "The purpose of this paper is therefore not to abandon the original violence–viability architecture, but to **deepen it**... Specifically, the analysis extends the framework in four key directions:
> 1. **Transmission Mechanisms** — Identifying the concrete pathways through which ecological stress, institutional design, and media environments influence population-level psychological states and collective behavior.
> 2. **Power and Political Economy** — Integrating the role of vested interests, regulatory capture, and structural incentives that can prevent societies from adapting even when solutions are available.
> 3. **Temporal Elasticity and Systemic Lag** — Introducing temporal dynamics that explain how civilizations can temporarily maintain stability while gradually eroding the foundations of their future viability.
> 4. **Empirically Grounded Pathways of Transformation** — Anchoring the HDT orientation in historical examples where institutional redesign successfully redirected societies away from cycles of violence."

### C-9 (p17, §1.4 closing — LOAD-BEARING) — Reframe of civilizational change
> "**Civilizations do not collapse or transform simply because ecological limits are crossed or cultural narratives shift.** They evolve through complex interactions among material constraints, institutional decisions, information systems, and power structures operating across time."

### C-10 (p18, §2.1 — LOAD-BEARING; DISTINCTIVE) — Distributed-physiological-entrainment
> "Rather than invoking a literal 'collective nervous system,' the phenomenon is better understood as **distributed physiological entrainment across populations**. This entrainment occurs through several interacting pathways."

### C-11 (p18-19, §2.2 — LOAD-BEARING) — Material conditions as physiological inputs (HPA-axis biological grounding)
> "Human physiology is directly influenced by environmental and economic conditions. Food security, housing stability, employment precarity, exposure to violence, public health conditions, and ecological disruption all affect baseline physiological regulation. Research in neurobiology and public health consistently shows that chronic stress alters hormonal systems, particularly through sustained activation of the **hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal (HPA) axis**. Elevated cortisol levels over long periods impair cognitive flexibility, increase threat vigilance, and reduce the capacity for cooperative problem-solving. **In other words, material insecurity directly conditions biological perception.**"
