## Verbatim load-bearing claims (selective-high-fidelity depth)

### C-1 (Sahely) — Civilizational paradox of wealth-amid-deprivation (Section I.1 opener)
> "Modern civilization has achieved unprecedented productive capacity. Global agriculture produces sufficient calories to feed the world's population. … Yet preventable deprivation persists. Across high-income and low-income nations alike, populations experience housing insecurity, untreated illness, food instability, energy poverty, water stress, and chronic economic precarity. These conditions are not limited to regions lacking physical resources; they appear within societies possessing abundant productive capacity." `[pdf-p10]`

### C-2 (Sahely) — Central structural question
> "When a society possesses the physical capacity to meet basic human needs, under what conditions does access to those necessities remain contingent, insecure, or denied? This inquiry does not begin with conspiracy, nor with ideological presupposition. It begins with a systems observation: persistent deprivation within conditions of aggregate capacity indicates the presence of gating mechanisms." `[pdf-p10]`

### C-3 (Sahely) — Four-principle Viability Architecture
> "The paper proposes a redesign framework grounded in four principles: 1. Secure a non-negotiable provisioning floor for life-necessities. 2. Align monetary issuance with real productive capacity. 3. Replace unemployment-based stabilization with employment-based mechanisms. 4. Integrate ecological ceilings into fiscal and credit systems." `[pdf-p9]`

### C-4 (Sahely) — Markets above provisioning floor (not anti-market)
> "Markets continue to operate above the provisioning floor. The redesign does not eliminate competition or private enterprise. It limits the domain in which survival insecurity functions as a disciplinary instrument." `[pdf-p9]`

### C-5 (Sahely) — Survival-insecurity as disciplinary instrument (novel framing)
> "[Viability redesign] limits the domain in which survival insecurity functions as a disciplinary instrument." `[pdf-p9]` — naming "survival insecurity as disciplinary instrument" is a Sahely-original framing that operationalizes McMurtry's substitution-trap / Civil-Commons critique at the level of monetary architecture

### C-6 (Sahely) — Transition sequencing (6 steps)
> "Reform must be sequenced and constraint-aware: De-weaponize essentials first. Replace discipline mechanisms gradually. Reorient credit incrementally. Embed ecological accounting institutionally. Preserve inflation monitoring and macroeconomic safeguards. Address external currency constraints pragmatically. Institutional change is evolutionary, not abrupt." `[pdf-p9]`

### C-7 (Sahely) — Civilizational thesis
> "Monetary architecture shapes legitimacy, conflict propensity, and long-term stability. Systems that stabilize themselves through managed vulnerability risk accumulating social fragility. Systems that secure survival access within ecological limits reduce enforcement pressure and strengthen legitimacy. The civilizational question is therefore not whether money is necessary. It is whether monetary systems are designed to stabilize life within limits — or to stabilize themselves through conditional scarcity." `[pdf-p9]`

### C-8 (Sahely) — "Conditional scarcity" diagnosis (novel framing)
> "[civilizational question:] whether monetary systems are designed to … stabilize themselves through conditional scarcity." `[pdf-p9]` — "conditional scarcity" is a Sahely-original concept-name that operationalizes the McMurtry artificial-vs-life-coherent distinction at the level of monetary architecture
