## The 3 closure motifs (canonical Sahely closure grammar)

Per cover-diagram + C-7 + Part III TOC:

1. **Loop Closure** (Ch 6) — closed compositions of a description must not drift; enforces quantization + anomaly cancellation + defect confinement. *Equivalent semantic: relational pathways through structure must agree when traversed back to start.*
2. **Junction Closure** (Ch 7) — local interactions must admit redundancy-invariant scalars; explains sparsity + rigidity of interaction grammars. *Equivalent semantic: at vertices where multiple relations meet, the joining must respect invariant structure.*
3. **Cut Closure** (Ch 8) — partitions between subsystems must respect finite informational capacity; gives rise to entropy bounds + emergent geometry + gravity-as-bookkeeping. **This is the sheaf-gluing-axiom in physics language.**

The chapter "**Exceptional Lie Groups as Redundancy Groups**" (§14, Part V) reframes E-series exceptional Lie groups as "redundancy groups" — groups that emerge as solutions to maximum-redundancy closure constraints. This is a *closure-rigid invariant theory* reading of E6/E7/E8.

The chapter "**Triality–G2 Closure Module**" (§19, Part VI) connects to the triality structure prominent in #1 / #2 / #4 / #5.
