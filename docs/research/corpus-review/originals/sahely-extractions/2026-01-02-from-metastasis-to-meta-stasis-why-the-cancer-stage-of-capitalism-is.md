## Verbatim key claims (~10 claims from pages 1-5)

### C-1 (p3, Dedication) — McMurtry framing (LOAD-BEARING)
> "This work is dedicated to **John McMurtry**, whose *The Cancer Stage of Capitalism* named — decades before it became unavoidable — the structural pathology now visible across biological, social, and planetary systems. McMurtry's insistence that value be judged by its effects on life capacity, rather than by money, power, or abstraction, provided the ethical and diagnostic clarity without which no cure is possible. What follows does not borrow his language as metaphor. It takes his diagnosis seriously as **structural fact**."

### C-2 (p3, Abstract) — Central thesis
> "Across biology, medicine, economics, and planetary governance, systems have become increasingly adaptive while simultaneously more fragile. This paper advances a unified regulatory framework explaining this paradox. It traces the evolutionary arc of regulation from **homeostasis** (stability through constancy) to **allostasis** (stability through change), identifies **metastasis** as the characteristic failure mode that emerges when adaptive power escapes governance, and introduces **meta-stasis** — stability through viability — as the missing regulatory layer required for recovery."

### C-3 (p3, Abstract) — Structural-exactness claim (LOAD-BEARING)
> "Drawing on cancer biology, stress physiology, systems theory, and life-value ethics, the paper demonstrates why John McMurtry's diagnosis of a 'cancer stage of capitalism' is not metaphorical but **structurally exact**. Healing is reframed as the recovery of jurisdiction: the restoration of the system's capacity to govern adaptation itself, protect buffers, enforce boundaries, and preserve future option space."

### C-4 (p4, Executive Summary) — 4-stage regulatory framework (CANONICAL)
> "This paper advances a regulatory synthesis organized around four stages:
> 1. **Homeostasis — stability through constancy.** Living systems regulate variables through corrective feedback. This works in stable environments but becomes brittle under sustained novelty or stress.
> 2. **Allostasis — stability through change.** Systems adapt by reconfiguring states predictively. This expands survivability but incurs costs. When allostatic states persist, buffers are depleted and allostatic load accumulates.
> 3. **Metastasis — adaptation without governance (failure mode).** When adaptation continues without a mechanism to decide when it must stop, systems enter a metastatic phase: unchecked expansion, boundary violation, buffer liquidation, and indifference to host viability. In biology, this is cancer. In political economy, it is the cancer stage of capitalism — structurally exact, not metaphorical.
> 4. **Meta-Stasis — stability through viability (cure).** Meta-stasis governs adaptation itself. It protects buffers before crisis, enforces jurisdictional boundaries, and preserves future option space. In organisms, it appears as immune surveillance, differentiation limits, and apoptotic exit. In societies, it appears as **rationing to life necessities, protection of the civil commons, and governance grounded in life-value criteria**. At the planetary scale, it requires treating Earth as a host system, not a resource pool."

### C-5 (p4, Executive Summary) — Healing reframing (LOAD-BEARING)
> "The paper reframes **healing** as the recovery of meta-stasis: not a return to equilibrium, but the restoration of the system's capacity to learn from stress and prevent its repetition. It reframes **freedom** as durable freedom — the preservation of choice across time — made possible only by early, intelligent constraint."

### C-6 (p4, Executive Summary) — Practical implication
> "Systems do not need less adaptation; they need **governed adaptation**. The transition from metastasis to meta-stasis is a mature evolutionary step — one that aligns power with protection, innovation with viability, and freedom with responsibility to time."

### C-7 (p5, Section 0) — Cancer-as-structural-precision (LOAD-BEARING)
> "A central claim of this work is that the language of pathology, particularly the language of cancer, is not being imported as metaphor. It is being used because it is precise. Cancer is not defined by growth alone, nor by error, nor by malign intent. It is defined by **adaptive activity that escapes the governance of the host**. When this pattern appears at the cellular level, we call it disease. When it appears at the social or economic level, we often call it success. This asymmetry is the conceptual blind spot the paper seeks to correct."

### C-8 (p5, Section 0) — Cross-scale inference discipline
> "Readers may initially find the extension of biological concepts into social and economic domains uncomfortable. That discomfort is appropriate. Cross-scale inference should never be casual. Throughout the work, such inference is justified not by analogy, but by **structural correspondence**: the same regulatory functions, failure modes, and recovery mechanisms recur wherever systems must survive their own power. Where the correspondence fails, it is acknowledged explicitly."

### C-9 (p5, Section 0) — Non-blame framing
> "This work is oriented toward healing rather than blame. Metastasis is not a moral failure; it is a regulatory one. Systems do not enter metastatic phases because they are evil, but because governance lags behind power."

### C-10 (p3, Abstract) — Integration claim
> "The framework integrates biological, social, and planetary scales into a single logic of solvency and offers a non-ideological pathway from crisis to cure grounded in the conditions by which life endures."
