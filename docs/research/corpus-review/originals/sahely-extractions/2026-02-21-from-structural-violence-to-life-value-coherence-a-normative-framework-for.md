## Verbatim key claims (~12 claims from pages 1-5)

### C-1 (p4, Abstract) — Civilizational paradox
> "Modern civilization exhibits a persistent paradox: expanding monetary growth and military capacity coexist with ecological degradation, widening inequality, and systemic public health instability. This paper advances a structural explanation. Violence is defined not merely as episodic conflict but as the avoidable reduction of life-capacity below materially attainable conditions due to institutional design."

### C-2 (p4, Abstract) — Structural-violence-as-emergent-property of value codes (LOAD-BEARING)
> "The analysis demonstrates how accumulation-centered value codes — equating rationality with monetary self-maximization — generate institutional structures that produce structural violence. Through five schematic models, the paper maps the causal architecture of this system, its recursive feedback insulation, its militarized security inversion, and its pathological growth dynamics."

### C-3 (p4, Abstract) — Life-Value reversal program
> "A life-value reversal is then articulated, redefining rationality as life-capacity enablement and proposing an operational Life-Capacity Audit Framework for institutional assessment. Crisis is modeled as a bifurcation point between retrenchment and revaluation."

### C-4 (p4, Abstract) — Framework purpose
> "The framework offers a coherent normative and diagnostic grammar for aligning economic, security, and governance systems with ecological stability and intergenerational viability."

### C-5 (p5, Exec Summary §Core Claim) — Violence definition (CANONICAL Galtungian formulation)
> "Violence is best understood as the **avoidable reduction of life-capacity** produced by institutional design. When rationality is equated with monetary self-maximization, institutions systematically degrade the life-support systems upon which civilization depends."

### C-6 (p5, Exec Summary §Structural Architecture) — Five structural claims (CANONICAL)
> "1. Governing value codes shape institutional design and life-capacity outcomes.
> 2. Structural violence reproduces itself through feedback insulation.
> 3. Militarized security often masks internal life-ground erosion.
> 4. Unregulated growth detached from ecological and social constraints mirrors pathological proliferation.
> 5. Crisis functions as a bifurcation threshold between retrenchment and revaluation."

### C-7 (p5, Exec Summary §Proposed Reversal) — Rationality redefinition (LOAD-BEARING)
> "A life-value framework redefines rationality as **life-capacity enablement**. Institutions are evaluated by whether they expand cognitive, biological, and social capacity across generations."

### C-8 (p5, Exec Summary §Proposed Reversal) — Three Life-Capacity Audit Dimensions (OPERATIONAL FRAMEWORK)
> "The Life-Capacity Institutional Audit Framework operationalizes this shift through measurable dimensions:
> • **Expansion of life-capacity**
> • **Protection of universal life necessities**
> • **Alignment of growth incentives with ecological constraint**"

### C-9 (p5, Exec Summary §Implications) — Durable peace requirement (LOAD-BEARING)
> "Durable peace requires structural coherence between institutional design and life-ground viability. Growth must be reintegrated within ecological and social constraints rather than detached from them."

### C-10 (p5, Exec Summary §Implications) — Civilizational choice (LOAD-BEARING)
> "The choice facing modern civilization is not growth versus stagnation, but **self-referential accumulation versus life-coherent viability**."

### C-11 (p2, TOC §6.3) — Structural Peace + Civil Commons (canonical)
Structural Peace and the Civil Commons paired as §6.3 — peace-research-discipline tradition (negative peace = absence of violence; positive peace = presence of justice; **structural peace = institutional design that prevents structural violence** at substrate layer).

### C-12 (p3, TOC Appendices F, G) — Bifurcation + Cancer-Analogy formalized
