## Verbatim key claims (16 claims; selective-high-fidelity)

### C-1 (p15, Abstract — LOAD-BEARING) — Operational thesis: relational-structure-degradation-as-failure-cause
> "Modern systems — clinical, ecological, economic, and infrastructural — often fail not because individual components break, but because **the relational structures that sustain them degrade**. This book develops a practical framework for understanding, detecting, and navigating such failures."

### C-2 (p15, Abstract — LOAD-BEARING) — Seven roles + triadic closure restated for practice
> "Building on a minimal relational grammar of **seven functional roles — Constraint, Margin, State, Disturbance, Perception, Regulation, and Options — and their organization into triadic closure, the work shows that viability depends on maintaining coherence across these interdependent relations**. When this coherence is disrupted, systems exhibit characteristic early warning signals: **path dependence, cross-channel divergence, increasing variability, and delayed recovery**."

### C-3 (p15, Abstract — LOAD-BEARING; DISTINCTIVE) — Control-strategy-worsens-instability claim
> "[The book] further demonstrates that **conventional control-based interventions often exacerbate instability by acting on observable projections rather than underlying structure**. In response, the text develops a mode of action based on **navigation rather than control — preserving margin, maintaining options, and aligning interventions with system dynamics**."

### C-4 (p16, Exec Summary — LOAD-BEARING) — Surface-projections vs relational-structure distinction
> "Traditional approaches focus on observable variables — vital signs, economic indicators, performance metrics — but **these capture only surface projections, not the relational structures that sustain system viability**."

### C-5 (p16, Exec Summary — LOAD-BEARING) — Central insight (LOAD-BEARING)
> "The central insight is simple but profound: **viability depends not on maintaining variables, but on preserving relational coherence**. Recognizing and acting on this principle enables earlier detection of failure, more effective intervention, and more resilient systems."

### C-6 (p17, Preface opening — LOAD-BEARING) — Triple-claim of the prior book
> "The first book established a claim. That **systems are not made of things, but of relations**. That **coherence arises not from components, but from how they are connected**. That **failure begins not in what we see, but in what we do not**. It was a book about structure. About inevitability. About the deep architecture that underlies systems across domains. But there was a question that remained. A necessary question. What do we do with this?"

### C-7 (p17, Preface — LOAD-BEARING; DISTINCTIVE) — Practitioner-orientation
> "Clinicians must make decisions at the bedside. Engineers must respond to system disturbances. Policymakers must act under uncertainty. Operators must manage real systems in real time. They do not have the luxury of abstraction alone. They need: **signals they can recognize, patterns they can interpret, actions they can take**."

### C-8 (p18, Preface — LOAD-BEARING) — Three operational questions (META-METHOD)
> "The first work demonstrated that relational coherence is fundamental. This work asks: **How does coherence break? How can we detect it early? How do we act without making things worse?**"

### C-9 (p18, Preface — DISTINCTIVE; honesty about limits) — What this book does NOT offer
> "[This book offers]: a way of seeing systems relationally / a method for detecting early signs of failure / a framework for interpreting those signals / a mode of action based on navigation rather than control. **It does not offer: certainty / complete prediction / guaranteed outcomes. It works within the limits of what can be known.**"
