## Verbatim key claims (~12 claims)

### C-1 (p7) — **"Fibered" operational definition (LOAD-BEARING — fiber-bundle substrate without requiring fiber-bundle mastery)**
> "The term **fibered** is used throughout this trilogy in a precise structural sense. It does not function as metaphor, decoration, or analogy. It names the way viability is organised whenever action must proceed through measurement. A fibered structure consists of three elements: 1. a **base space**, 2. a **fiber** over each point of the base, and 3. a **projection** that connects them."

### C-2 (p7) — **Base space + fiber + projection across three scales (LOAD-BEARING — cross-scale grammar template)**
> "The **base space** is the domain of what can be stabilised, shared, and acted upon. In physics, it consists of observables and measurement outcomes. In medicine, it consists of biomarkers, diagnoses, and targets. In governance, it consists of indicators, accounts, and policy metrics. The base space is indispensable. Without it, coordination is impossible. The **fiber** is the domain of hidden coherence. It contains the relational structure that cannot be fully projected into stable variables without loss. In physics, this appears as non-commuting structure, symmetry, and invariant relations. In medicine, it appears as physiological reserve, adaptive capacity, and recovery dynamics. In governance, it appears as civil commons, ecological regeneration, and institutional trust."

### C-3 (p7) — **Projection necessary-but-non-invertible (LOAD-BEARING)**
> "Projection maps the fibered structure into the base space. This map is **necessary but non-invertible**. Information is lost in projection, not because of error, but because stability requires compression. What matters is not eliminating this loss, but respecting its consequences."

### C-4 (p7) — Viability operational definition
> "A system is **viable** when action in the base space remains compatible with the coherence preserved in the fiber. Failure occurs when projected measures are treated as exhaustive representations of reality and optimisation proceeds without regard to the relations that sustain the system."

### C-5 (p7) — Scale-recurrence as central organizing claim
> "The trilogy is therefore *fibered* because its central concern — viability — does not reside in what is measured, but in what must be preserved beneath measurement. The same structure recurs across scales. The electron, the patient, and the nation each exhibit a base of observables, a fiber of coherence, and invariant constraints that regulate admissible interaction between them."

### C-6 (p7) — **Anti-metaphor declaration (LOAD-BEARING — structural-not-decorative use of fiber)**
> "Nothing in this work requires mastery of mathematical fiber bundles. The term *fibered* is used because it names, with minimal abstraction, a structure that is already doing explanatory work: action proceeds in the base, but endurance is decided in the fiber."

### C-7 (p8, Abstract) — Central thesis
> "Across physics, medicine, and governance, systems increasingly succeed by their own metrics while failing to endure. Precision improves, control tightens, and indicators look better — yet coherence erodes and collapse arrives abruptly. This trilogy argues that these failures share a common structural cause: a persistent confusion between *projection* and *reality*."

### C-8 (p8, Abstract) — Fibered viability central claim (LOAD-BEARING — composes with #1 sheaf framing)
> "Measurement is indispensable, but it is never exhaustive. Action proceeds through stabilised variables — observables, biomarkers, indicators — while the conditions for persistence reside in relational structures that cannot be fully projected without loss. This work names that structure as **fibered viability**: systems act in a measurable base space, but remain viable only if hidden coherence in the fiber is preserved."

### C-9 (p9, Preface) — Origin from cross-domain pattern recognition
> "This trilogy did not begin as a theory. It began as a recurring discomfort — encountered in different domains, at different scales, and in different professional lives — that systems were increasingly successful by their own measures while becoming less able to endure. Precision improved. Control tightened. Evidence accumulated. Yet fragility grew. [...] The same pattern appeared wherever measurement became the primary interface with reality."

### C-10 (p9, Preface) — Central claim distillation (LOAD-BEARING)
> "The central claim of this trilogy is that **viability is governed not by what we optimise, but by what we preserve**."

### C-11 (p9-10, Preface) — Electron/patient/nation as instances not analogies (LOAD-BEARING)
> "The trilogy is organised as three volumes not because the domains are equivalent, but because the structure recurs. The electron, the patient, and the nation are not analogies; they are instances. Each reveals the same grammar: action in a measurable base space, endurance governed by hidden coherence, and failure when coupling between the two is unbounded."

### C-12 (p13, Executive Summary) — Fibered viability across three volumes (LOAD-BEARING)
> "The trilogy introduces **fibered viability** as a unifying structural template. In a fibered system, action occurs in a base space of measurable projections, while endurance is governed by a distinct layer of hidden coherence. Viability depends on maintaining bounded coupling between these layers. Excessive control erodes coherence; insufficient openness prevents adaptation."
