## Verbatim load-bearing claims (REPOSTED-curated — attributed to McMurtry 1989)

### MW-1 (McMurtry 1989, abstracted) — Military Paradigm as reductive
> "The Military Paradigm is Reductive: It falsely equates 'war' exclusively with mass homicide, ignoring non-violent or species-defensive modes of conflict." `[pdf-p3, NotebookLM abstracting McMurtry 1989]`

### MW-2 (McMurtry 1989, abstracted) — Revolutionary change in sentiment
> "The document identifies a revolutionary change in human sentiment: while historical cultures glorified mass homicide, contemporary humanity largely abhors the mechanized routines of social murder. However, political and economic structures remain 'locked' into a homicidal framework because it serves the interests of ruling elites." `[pdf-p3]`

### MW-3 (McMurtry 1989, abstracted) — Pathological vs Enabling War
> "The analysis distinguishes between 'pathological war' (the organized killing of humans) and 'enabling war' (organized campaigns against non-human threats like disease, destitution, and environmental degradation)." `[pdf-p3]`

### MW-4 (McMurtry 1989, abstracted) — Constructed Enemies
> "Constructed Enemies: The 'national enemy' is often a political construction used to justify militarization, which frequently poses a greater threat to a nation's own citizens than any foreign power." `[pdf-p3]`

### MW-5 (McMurtry 1989, abstracted) — Political Economy of Arms
> "Political Economy of Arms: The arms race functions as a sophisticated economic engine, benefiting from high per-unit prices, rapid obsolescence, and state-guaranteed financing." `[pdf-p3]`

### MW-6 (McMurtry 1989, abstracted) — Agent/Structure Distinction
> "The Agent/Structure Distinction: A 'just war' should target disabling social patterns and coercive structures rather than the individual human beings (soldiers or civilians) who are often involuntary participants in the military system." `[pdf-p3]`
