## Verbatim key claims (15 claims; selective-high-fidelity)

### C-1 (p7, Abstract — LOAD-BEARING) — Cross-domain collapse pattern
> "Complex adaptive systems across domains — including biological organisms, ecological communities, financial networks, and geopolitical institutions — exhibit a common pattern of sudden collapse following extended periods of apparent stability. Traditional analyses often focus on individual variables within these systems, yet such variables frequently fail to capture the structural conditions that determine persistence under disturbance."

### C-2 (p7, Abstract — LOAD-BEARING; DISTINCTIVE) — Seven informational roles + Fano plane
> "This paper proposes a minimal relational framework for analyzing viability in complex adaptive systems. The framework identifies **seven informational roles — constraints, margins, system state, disturbances, perception, regulation, and optionality — that together form the minimal architecture required for persistence**. These roles interact through a set of **seven triadic relations that correspond to the unique Steiner triple system S(2,3,7), represented by the Fano plane**."

### C-3 (p7, Abstract — LOAD-BEARING) — Geometric collapse interpretation
> "This relational grammar generates a **geometric representation of system dynamics in which persistence corresponds to trajectories remaining within a viable region of state space defined by constraints and margins**. Collapse occurs when margins erode and optional future trajectories disappear."

### C-4 (p7, Abstract — LOAD-BEARING; DISTINCTIVE) — Exceptional algebraic structures connection (HIGHLY SPECULATIVE)
> "The resulting framework provides a unified perspective on fragility, resilience, and systemic collapse. **The appearance of the Fano combinatorial structure suggests deeper connections with exceptional algebraic systems such as the octonions and the Freudenthal triple system associated with the exceptional Lie group E_7**. While these mathematical correspondences are presented primarily as scaffolding for future research, they highlight the possibility that persistence in complex adaptive systems may depend on maintaining coherence within a minimal relational architecture."

### C-5 (p26, §5 opening — LOAD-BEARING) — Seven roles as functional positions
> "Across biological, ecological, economic, and institutional systems, seven such roles appear consistently. These roles **do not correspond to specific mechanisms or substances. Rather, they represent functional positions within the architecture of persistence**. The seven roles are: constraints, margins, system state, disturbances, perception, regulation, and optionality."

### C-6 (p26, §5 Constraints — LOAD-BEARING) — Constraint definition (without constraints, no viability)
> "Constraints define the boundaries within which a system can continue to function... Constraints therefore **specify the limits of admissible system states. Without constraints, the concept of viability would have no meaning. A system cannot fail if no limits exist.**"

### C-7 (p27, §5 Margins) — Margins as buffer distance
> "Margins represent the distance between the system's current state and the nearest constraint boundary... **Margins provide the buffer that allows systems to absorb disturbances without crossing failure thresholds. When margins decline, systems become increasingly sensitive to perturbations.**"

### C-8 (p28, §5 Perception — LOAD-BEARING) — Perception layer (cross-domain)
> "Perception refers to the mechanisms through which a system acquires information about its condition and environment. In biological organisms, perception occurs through sensory and biochemical signaling systems. In ecosystems, perception may occur through distributed feedback processes among species and environmental variables. **In human institutions, perception occurs through monitoring systems, scientific observation, and information networks. Perception allows the system to detect disturbances and estimate its proximity to constraint boundaries. Without perception, regulatory responses become blind.**"

### C-9 (p28, §5 Optionality — LOAD-BEARING; DISTINCTIVE) — Optionality as space-of-possible-futures
> "**Optionality represents the number of viable trajectories available to the system.** A system with high optionality possesses many potential pathways through which it can respond to disturbances while remaining within the viable region of its state space. In ecosystems, optionality emerges from biodiversity and functional redundancy. In economic systems, it appears through diversified supply chains and multiple sources of credit. In physiological systems, optionality may appear as alternative metabolic pathways or therapeutic interventions. Optionality therefore represents **the space of possible futures available to the system. When optionality collapses, the system may remain temporarily stable but loses the capacity to adapt to new disturbances.**"

### C-10 (p29, §5 Architecture of Persistence — LOAD-BEARING summary) — Seven-role minimal architecture
> "Constraints define the limits of admissible states. System state indicates where the system currently lies. Margins measure the distance from those limits. Disturbances push the system through its state space. Perception detects those changes. Regulation attempts to counteract them. Optionality determines whether alternative trajectories remain available. **Together these roles form the minimal informational architecture through which adaptive systems maintain viability under disturbance.**"

### C-11 (p31, §6 — LOAD-BEARING; DISTINCTIVE) — Three ontological classes
> "Closer examination reveals that the seven roles naturally partition into **three ontological classes: world-facing roles, system-facing roles, and mediating roles of agency**. [§6.1-6.5: world-facing = constraints + disturbances; system-facing = system-state + margins + optionality; mediating-of-agency = perception + regulation.]"
