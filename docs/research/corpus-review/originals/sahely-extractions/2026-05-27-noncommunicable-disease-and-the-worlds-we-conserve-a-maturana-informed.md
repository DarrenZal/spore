## Verbatim key claims

### C-1 (p2, title-page callout) — Core thesis (LOAD-BEARING)
> "Core thesis: NCDs are best understood not only as chronic failures inside individual bodies, but as conserved organism–niche miscouplings — patterns of living in which biological, emotional, relational, commercial, ecological, and institutional conditions recursively stabilize disease-producing trajectories." [anchor: §Title-page · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p2]

### C-2 (p5, Abstract) — Risk-factor frame is indispensable but incomplete
> "That framing remains indispensable for surveillance and evidence-based clinical care. Yet it is incomplete when it treats the body as the primary site of disease while under-describing the recurrent organism–niche relations through which chronic disease patterns are generated, conserved, and transmitted across daily life." [anchor: §Abstract · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p5]

### C-3 (p5, Abstract) — NCDs as stabilized miscouplings; does not replace biomedicine
> "It proposes that many NCD patterns can be understood as stabilized organism–niche miscouplings: conserved ways of living in which biological, emotional, relational, commercial, ecological, and institutional conditions recursively reproduce disease-producing trajectories. The framework does not replace biomedical diagnosis, pharmacotherapy, surgery, oncology, renal medicine, emergency care, or public-health best buys. It places those interventions within a wider biology of living in which healing requires transformations in the relational conditions that make healthier patterns of living possible." [anchor: §Abstract · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p5]

### C-4 (p5, Exec Summary) — Autopoietic unity definition
> "A Maturana-informed view begins from the living organism as an autopoietic unity: a self-producing, history-shaped being that conserves itself through recurrent structural coupling with its niche (Maturana Romesín & Varela, 1980)." [anchor: §Executive-Summary · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p5]

### C-5 (p5, Exec Summary) — Diseases of conserved maladaptive coherence (LOAD-BEARING)
> "From this standpoint, many NCD patterns are diseases of conserved maladaptive coherence. The organism is not merely malfunctioning. It may be surviving under constraint. Hypertension, insulin resistance, chronic inflammation, central adiposity, anxiety eating, alcohol dependence, sedentary exhaustion, and sleep disruption may be adaptive responses that become pathological when the organism remains locked into a disease-producing niche." [anchor: §Executive-Summary · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p5]

### C-6 (p5, Exec Summary) — The clinical-question shift (LOAD-BEARING; callout box)
> "The clinical question shifts from 'Why is this patient non-compliant?' to 'What organism–niche pattern is this person conserving, and what new relational conditions would allow a healthier way of living to become possible?'" [anchor: §Executive-Summary · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p5]

### C-7 (p6, Introduction) — Choices arise within worlds
> "A Maturana-informed view asks whether chronic disease persists because the organism and its niche are locked into a recurrent pattern that conserves disease-producing living. Choices arise within worlds: emotional worlds, economic worlds, food worlds, work worlds, family worlds, commercial worlds, climatic worlds, and institutional worlds. These worlds shape what actions are visible, feasible, meaningful, and sustainable." [anchor: §1-Introduction · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p6]

### C-8 (p6, Scope and Safeguard) — Two commitments held together
> "The paper therefore holds two commitments together: clinical seriousness and relational depth. Biomedical care and niche transformation are not rivals. They are complementary moments in life-serving medicine." [anchor: §2-Scope-and-Safeguard · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p6]

### C-9 (p6-7, Limits of the Risk-Factor Model) — Moralization critique
> "The limitation is not that the model is wrong. The limitation is that it can become too narrow when treated as a complete account of chronic disease. It may name what is associated with disease without explaining how disease-producing patterns are conserved in everyday life. It can also unintentionally moralize chronic disease by converting structurally shaped patterns into individual failures." [anchor: §3-Limits-of-Risk-Factor-Model · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p6]

### C-10 (p7, Limits — callout) — Risk factors are indicators not explanations
> "Risk factors remain necessary indicators, but they are not yet explanations of the world that conserves them." [anchor: §3-Limits-of-Risk-Factor-Model · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p7]

### C-11 (p7, Maturana's Biology of Living — Structural coupling) — Coupling can be health- or disease-generating
> "Through recurrent interactions, organism and environment become fitted to each other. Coupling can be health-generating or disease-generating. Eating patterns, sleep rhythms, stress responses, movement habits, and emotional coping strategies emerge through repeated coupling with family, school, work, culture, economy, ecology, and care systems." [anchor: §4-Maturanas-Biology-of-Living · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p7]

### C-12 (p7, Biology of Living — Emotioning) — Emotioning specifies domains of action
> "Emotions are bodily-relational dispositions that specify domains of possible action. A person in shame, fear, grief, exhaustion, trust, dignity, or love inhabits different worlds of possible conduct. Information alone rarely changes behavior unless the emotional field of possibility changes." [anchor: §4-Maturanas-Biology-of-Living · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p7]

### C-13 (p7, Biology of Living — Languaging) — Clinical language can close or open possibility
> "Human beings live in language as a domain of coordination. Phrases such as 'I am non-compliant,' 'I have no willpower,' or 'nothing works' are not neutral; they can close possibility. Clinical language can either deepen shame or open a new domain of action." [anchor: §4-Maturanas-Biology-of-Living · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p7]

### C-14 (p7, Biology of Living — The biology of love) — Love as relational ground (LOAD-BEARING for care-commoning bridge)
> "Love, in Maturana's biological sense, is the relational domain in which the other is allowed to appear as a legitimate other in coexistence. In NCD care, dignity, respect, and trust are not soft extras; they widen the patient's domain of possible action." [anchor: §4-Maturanas-Biology-of-Living · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p7]

### C-15 (p7, What Maturana Adds) — Biology of relationality
> "Maturana's contribution must be stated plainly: he gives a biology of relationality—a way to understand how social, emotional, material, linguistic, commercial, and ecological conditions become embodied as patterns of living." ### C-16 (p8, What Maturana Adds) — A theory of how living beings conserve worlds
> "The distinctive contribution is not another list of determinants. It is a theory of how living beings conserve worlds. NCDs persist because certain worlds of living are repeatedly brought forth and stabilized: worlds of cheap calories and expensive health, vigilance without recovery, shame and avoidance, fragmented care, commercial seduction, climate stress, family strain, and narrowed possibility." [anchor: §5-What-Maturana-Adds · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p8]

### C-17 (p8, Organism–Niche Coherence and Miscoupling) — Coherence is not comfort (LOAD-BEARING)
> "Coherence is not mere comfort, stability, or adaptation. A person may be adapted to a harmful situation. A family may be stable but oppressive. A food system may be efficient but pathogenic. Coherence must therefore be judged by whether the conserved pattern expands or narrows life-capacity." [anchor: §6-Organism-Niche-Coherence-and-Miscoupling · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p8]

> "Organism–niche miscoupling occurs when recurrent relations conserve patterns that allow short-term adaptation but progressively narrow the organism's range of viable action, producing chronic physiological, behavioral, emotional, and relational strain." ### The CARE Method (s10, p14) — verbatim four-step clinical sequence

> "The CARE Method translates the framework into a practical clinical sequence."
> - **C — Contextualize the condition** — "Ask: What is the patient's lived world? This includes disease history, daily routines, emotional field, family context, work and time pressures, food environment, financial constraints, language, beliefs, and care experience."
> - **A — Assess conserved patterns** — "Ask: What pattern keeps repeating across body, emotion, family, work, and niche? The aim is not blame but recognition of the recurrent loop that conserves disease."
> - **R — Re-open a feasible domain of action** — "Ask: What small non-forcing change can become livable? The aim is one feasible change that alters the coupling between organism and niche."
> - **E — Embed and evaluate** — "Ask: What support will help the new pattern be conserved over time? Use follow-up, reminders, family support, community resources, medication access, monitoring, and adaptation. Setbacks are treated as information, not failure."
> [anchor: §10-CARE-Method · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p14]

### Figure 4 — Care Pathway (6 stages, p15, verbatim from diagram)

1. RELATIONAL ENTRY & SECOND-ORDER LISTENING — Welcome the person, not the disease; elicit lived experience, concerns, goals; explore meanings/emotions/strengths/constraints; co-create the purpose of the encounter.
2. COMPREHENSIVE COHERENCE ASSESSMENT — Use the NCD Coherence Map (Figure 2) across 8 domains; identify protective factors and stressors; map key miscouplings and resources; prioritize with the person what matters most.
3. SHARED UNDERSTANDING & CO-CREATED PLAN — Explain conditions in everyday language; link biology with life context; agree on meaningful goals (SMART-ER¹); co-design a stepwise plan small, doable, and reversible.
4. INTEGRATED INTERVENTIONS — Evidence-based biomedical care; behavior & lifestyle support; emotional & mental well-being; family & social connection; practical supports (food, movement, sleep, work, finance); address structural constraints.
5. CONTINUOUS RELATIONAL COUPLING & ADAPTATION — Regular follow-up & monitoring; review what is working and what is not; adapt the plan with the person; reinforce identity as a capable agent of change; strengthen support networks.
6. LIFE-COHERENT OUTCOMES — Improved biomarkers and symptoms; better quality of life and functioning; greater resilience and life meaning; reduced complications and health-system use; contribution to family and community well-being.
> ¹ SMART-ER Goals: Specific, Meaningful, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound, Evaluated, and Reversible. ² UPP: Ultra-processed products. [anchor: §Figure-4 · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-noncommunicable-disease-and-the-worlds-w · pdf-p15]
