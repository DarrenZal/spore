## Verbatim key claims

### C-1 (p3, Abstract) — Problem statement (LOAD-BEARING)
> "Contemporary societies face a persistent paradox: despite widespread commitment to values such as health, prosperity, freedom, and sustainability, social, ecological, and human crises continue to deepen. This white paper argues that the problem lies not in the absence of values, but in the absence of a shared, life-grounded standard for what value is."

### C-2 (p3, Abstract) — Ethics-as-viability-science thesis (CENTRAL THESIS; LOAD-BEARING for Spore F8 + F5/F6 doctrine layer)
> "Drawing on Life-Value Onto-Axiology, developed by John McMurtry, the paper reframes ethics as a science of viability — the systematic inquiry into what allows living systems to continue, adapt, and flourish without self-destruction. At its core is the Primary Axiom of Value, which defines value as the expansion of the coherent range of thought, feeling, and action, and disvalue as their reduction or destruction."

### C-3 (p3, Abstract) — Framework unfolding (chain of 7 principles)
> "The paper unfolds this axiom step by step into universal human life necessities, life-coherent principles of social and economic organization, measures of sufficiency and progress rooted in civil commons development, the concept of life capital, and life-value efficiency criteria that prevent short-term gains from eroding long-term capacity. Ethics, economics, public health, and ecology are shown to share a single underlying logic: life must be organized so that its enabling conditions are preserved and enhanced over time."

### C-4 (p4, Executive Summary) — Primary Axiom of Value (verbatim)
> "Developed by John McMurtry, this framework defines value not in terms of price, preference, or belief, but in relation to life's basic requirements. The Primary Axiom of Value states that something is valuable if and only if it expands the coherent range of human thought, feeling, and action, and disvaluable insofar as it diminishes these capacities. This axiom functions as a universal orientation, applicable across cultures, domains, and historical contexts."

### C-5 (p4, Executive Summary) — Universal Human Life Necessities
> "Universal human life necessities — such as clean air and water, nourishing food, shelter, care, education, meaningful work, and social inclusion — are identified as the material and social conditions required for life capacities to exist at all. Social and economic success is therefore redefined in terms of secure access to these necessities, rather than aggregate monetary growth."

### C-6 (p4, Executive Summary) — Closed testable system
> "Progress is assessed through the development of civil commons rather than GDP; capital is understood as life capital that regenerates rather than depletes; and efficiency is defined by ecological integrity, reduced material and energy inputs, and the expansion of human capability. Together, these elements form a closed, testable system in which ethical claims are evaluated against observable life outcomes."

### C-7 (p4, Executive Summary) — Science-of-viability framing
> "By integrating ethics, economics, health, and ecology within a single life-coherent logic, the paper reframes ethics as a science of viability — the disciplined study of what allows living systems to persist and flourish without destroying their own foundations. The result is not a political program or moral doctrine, but a shared compass for navigating complex decisions in a finite world."

### C-8 (p5, §1 The Problem) — Diagnosis of detachment from life
> "In modern thought, value has been progressively detached from life itself. In economics, value is often equated with price and market demand. In ethics, it is treated as a matter of preference, duty, or abstract principle. In policy, it is reduced to targets, metrics, or growth indicators. Each of these approaches captures something partial, but none provides a stable standard for judging whether social arrangements are actually supporting or undermining the conditions of human life over time."

### C-9 (p17, §11 Constraint Closure) — Falsifiability anchor (LOAD-BEARING for cross-tradition support of Spore F8 external-validation-loop)
> "Because the framework is grounded in observable life conditions, it is not immune to falsification. If a policy, institution, or economic arrangement claims to support life but produces worsening health, rising deprivation, ecological damage, or shrinking human agency, the contradiction is not ideological — it is empirical. The system has violated its own criteria."

### C-10 (p17, §11) — Avoidance of relativism AND dogmatism
> "This is why Life-Value Onto-Axiology avoids both relativism and dogmatism. It does not depend on shared beliefs, moral consensus, or cultural agreement. Nor does it impose fixed rules regardless of context. Instead, it operates as a **living evaluative system**, continuously tested against the realities of life itself. Where life-range expands sustainably, the framework is confirmed. Where it contracts, correction is required."

### C-11 (p17, §11) — Ethics as answerable to life-viability
> "In this way, ethics becomes answerable — not to authority, tradition, or preference — but to the ongoing viability of living systems."

### C-12 (p18, §12 Ethics Reframed) — Ethics-as-viability-science definition
> "Seen as a whole, Life-Value Onto-Axiology reframes the nature of ethics itself. Ethics is no longer primarily about adjudicating between competing moral claims or prescribing ideal behavior. It becomes the study of **what allows living systems to continue, adapt, and flourish without self-destruction**."

### C-13 (p18, §12) — Immanent ethics (LOAD-BEARING — composes with Spore ADR-0042 structural-legitimacy)
> "From this perspective, ethics is not an external constraint placed on systems after the fact. It is **immanent** in whether systems work at all over time. A society that persistently violates life-value principles will eventually face rising instability, conflict, and collapse — not as punishment, but as consequence."

### C-14 (p18, §12) — Responsibility as stewardship of conditions
> "This also changes the meaning of responsibility. Responsibility is no longer primarily about individual virtue or blame. It is about stewardship of the conditions that make value possible. Those with decision-making power — whether in governments, corporations, or institutions — are responsible to the extent that their choices preserve or degrade life capital and life-range for present and future generations."

### C-15 (p18, §12) — Systems-designed-life-coherent-even-imperfect (LOAD-BEARING for Spore F3 actor-governance ADR-0077 + F5 actuator-logic ADR-0076)
> "Importantly, this reframing does not demand perfect people. It recognizes that human beings are fallible, self-interested, and limited in foresight. What it requires instead are **systems designed to be life-coherent even when people are imperfect** — systems that align incentives, structures, and feedback with the conditions of viability itself."

### C-16 (p19, §13 Conclusion) — Shared-ground proposal
> "At the same time, it offers something constructive and rare: a **shared ground** beneath ethical disagreement. By rooting value in the universal requirements of life, it allows societies to evaluate their arrangements without resorting to ideology, moralism, or cultural superiority. The question is always the same and always answerable: *does this way of organizing life expand or contract the real capacities of living beings over time?*"

### C-17 (p19, §13) — Closing line (highest leverage)
> "When societies align with this logic, progress becomes durable. When they do not, collapse is only delayed. That is the quiet but uncompromising insight at the heart of Life-Value Onto-Axiology: **value is not what we say we care about, but what keeps the conditions of caring alive**."
