## Verbatim load-bearing claims (Sahely-AUTHORED, hand-curated for KG)

### S5-1 (Central thesis, single-principle summary)
> "A civilization becomes worthy of survival when its deepest symbols are interpreted not as licenses for sacrifice, but as obligations to protect the conditions of life for all." `[pdf-p11, §"Toward a Reflexive Civilization"]`

### S5-2 (Galtung 3-violence triangle as analytical frame)
> "Drawing on the peace research framework developed by Johan Galtung, the paper distinguishes three interacting forms of violence. Direct violence consists of visible acts of physical harm, such as warfare and terrorism. Structural violence refers to institutional arrangements that systematically produce deprivation, insecurity, or unequal access to life-supporting resources. Cultural violence consists of symbolic narratives that legitimize or normalize these forms of harm." `[pdf-p9, §"Violence as a System"]`

### S5-3 (Civilizations as narrative organisms)
> "Civilizations are therefore not merely systems of governance or production; they are narrative organisms that interpret reality through symbolic frameworks inherited across generations." `[pdf-p14, Ch 1]`

### S5-4 (Life-Ground Criterion from McMurtry)
> "To address this question, the paper draws on the life-ground philosophy developed by John McMurtry. According to this framework, social systems should be evaluated according to whether they sustain the conditions necessary for human life and dignity. These conditions include access to water, food, health care, shelter, education, ecological stability, and meaningful social participation." `[pdf-p10, §"The Life-Ground Criterion"]`

### S5-5 (Viability Geometry Model — three-axis framework)
> "The paper introduces the Viability Geometry Model, a systems framework describing how civilizations navigate constraints, margins, and optionality. Constraints represent fundamental limits imposed by ecological and social realities. Margins represent the buffer zones protecting societies from crisis. Optionality represents the range of viable responses available to political decision-makers." `[pdf-p10, §"The Viability Geometry Model"]`

### S5-6 (Violence as Optionality Collapse)
> "Violence often corresponds to a collapse of optionality. When societies perceive their choices as limited to domination or destruction, escalation becomes more likely." `[pdf-p10]` — **Key novel concept**: violence as a structural-consequence of optionality narrowing, not a moral category alone.

### S5-7 (Girardian scapegoat mechanism)
> "The philosopher René Girard argued that societies often stabilize internal tensions by directing violence toward symbolic victims. Entire populations may become associated with threat or guilt, allowing their suffering to be interpreted as necessary for preserving order." `[pdf-p10, §"The Scapegoat Mechanism"]`

### S5-8 (Cross as civilizational diagnostic symbol — Girardian inversion)
> "The crucifixion of Jesus Christ provides a symbolic lens for examining this phenomenon. Historically an act of Roman state violence, the crucifixion has been interpreted both as sacrificial redemption and as a revelation of the injustice of scapegoating systems. This tension illustrates a broader civilizational question: whether social order requires sacrificial victims, or whether societies can organize themselves around protecting the vulnerable." `[pdf-p10, §"The Cross as Civilizational Diagnostic Symbol"]`

### S5-9 (Civilizational Regulation Model — homeostasis / allostasis / adaptation / reflexivity / regression)
> "[Ch 7] Homeostasis: Maintaining Immediate Stability … Allostasis: Crisis and Emergency Response … Adaptation: Institutional Transformation … Reflexivity: Civilizational Self-Examination … Regression and the Dynamics of Crisis" `[pdf-p39-42, Ch 7 — Sahely's systems-biology framework applied to civilizations]`

### S5-10 (HDT framework — Holism-Dialectics-Transcendence)
> "Drawing on Galtung's broader framework of Holism-Dialectics-Transcendence … Holism: Seeing the Whole System / Dialectics: Recognizing Multiple Historical Truths / Transcendence: Moving Beyond the Structure of the Conflict" `[pdf-p49-51, Ch 9]`

### S5-11 (Five narrative transformations — Ch 10)
> "Chapter 10 — Rewriting the Moral Story of Civilization: From Chosenness to Responsibility / From Sacrifice to Protection / From Enemy Metaphysics to Human Complexity / From Grievance Memory to Ethical Memory / From Victory to Flourishing" `[pdf-p53-55, Ch 10]`

### S5-12 (Middle East case study — five-actor viability map)
> "Applying this framework to the present war system involving Israel, Gaza, Iran, and regional actors reveals a multi-layered structure of conflict. Gaza currently operates near or below the humanitarian viability threshold … Israel retains strong institutional capacity but faces intense security pressures and narrowing narrative options … Iran operates under heavy external constraint yet retains strategic depth. Regional states experience varying degrees of vulnerability." `[pdf-p11, §"Case Study"]` + Appendix L Viability Map (pp 125-130) ranks Gaza / Israel / Iran / Lebanon-Hezbollah / Gulf states / United States / Energy systems along the viability axis.
