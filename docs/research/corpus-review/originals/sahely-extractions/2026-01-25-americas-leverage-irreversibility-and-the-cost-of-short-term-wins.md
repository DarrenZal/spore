## Verbatim key claims (7 claims; selective depth — fully covered from email body)

### C-1 (email body, "core thesis") — Momentum-not-intention as governing dynamic (LOAD-BEARING; viability-grammar substrate; resonates with Spore F6 failure-modes + ADR-0046 Field substrate)
> "Some systems don't respond to intentions — they respond to momentum.
> If you're moving too fast toward a wall, turning the wheel isn't enough. You need braking distance.
> 
> Right now, the world is still accelerating toward several hard boundaries — climate instability, ecosystem collapse, pandemic risk, and global fragmentation — while weakening the very braking systems that would let us slow down later.
> 
> That's the danger zone. Not collapse tomorrow — inevitability later."

### C-2 (email body, "winning the long run") — Short-run strength vs long-run viability tradeoff (load-bearing for viability budget framing)
> "You can look strong in the short run while losing the ability to win in the long run."

### C-3 (email body, "Energy dominance without braking") — Atmospheric-threshold irreversibility / commitments-you-can't-negotiate-with
> "Expanding energy supply feels like strength — and it is, short term. But if total emissions keep rising, atmospheric levels keep climbing no matter who drills. That creates momentum you can't negotiate with later. Once certain thresholds are crossed, damages compound automatically. That's not theory — it's physics."

### C-4 (email body, "Withdrawing from global coordination") — Coordination-as-redundancy framing (resonates with Spore ADR-0079 F9 maintenance-economics + reproductive-infrastructure)
> "Leaving institutions may feel like reclaiming sovereignty, but in pandemics, climate shocks, and financial contagion, coordination is not charity — it's redundancy. Removing redundancy doesn't make you freer; it makes failure more expensive when it hits."

### C-5 (email body, "Burning natural capital") — Shock-absorber framing of ecological infrastructure (resonates with Spore ADR-0079 F9 + ADR-0073 F1 sensor-oracle for ecological-state)
> "Forests, soils, water systems — these are not environmental luxuries. They are shock absorbers. Lose them, and disasters get bigger, recovery gets slower, insurance collapses, and governments pay more forever."

### C-6 (email body, "Running systems hot") — Slack/buffers/recoverability as real strength (LOAD-BEARING for viability-grammar; sibling to Wave-1 sheaf-substrate option-space concept)
> "When everything works only at full throttle — health systems, supply chains, geopolitics — you have no margin. Real strength shows up as slack, buffers, and recoverability. Right now, those are thinning."

### C-7 (email body, "strategic mistake") — Confusing present control with future freedom; option-space depletion as systemic failure-mode (LOAD-BEARING; viability-grammar substrate)
> "The biggest error great powers make is confusing present control with future freedom.
> 
> Destroying future options to win today is how empires corner themselves.
> 
> Once certain boundaries are crossed, no president, no technology, no deal can undo them on meaningful timescales. That's what 'irreversible' actually means."
