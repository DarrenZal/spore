### C-1 (Abstract, p2) — Central derivational thesis (LOAD-BEARING)
> "This white paper proposes such a framework by deriving neuropsychiatric disease from disturbances in living coherence across five foundational layers: autopoiesis, structural coupling, emotioning, felt interiority, and emotional sentience." ### C-2 (Abstract, p2) — Five-layer definitions
> "Autopoiesis defines the living organism as a materially open, organizationally closed, self-producing unity. Structural coupling situates this unity within histories of viable or destructive relation with its medium. Emotioning specifies bodily domains of possible action through which organisms meet the world. Felt sense and interoception disclose the organism's living condition from within. Emotional sentience names the evaluative self-regulatory dimension by which life feels how life is going." [anchor: §Abstract · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-living-coherence-and-neuropsychiatric-di · pdf-p2]

### C-3 (Abstract, p2) — Incoherence-as-nonviability reframe (LOAD-BEARING; de-moralizing move)
> "In this usage, incoherence is not a personal failing or moral deficit; it is a sign that life conditions, regulatory capacity, or organism-world coupling have become nonviable." [anchor: §Abstract · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-living-coherence-and-neuropsychiatric-di · pdf-p2]

### C-4 (Abstract, p2) — Seven-Primitives Clinical Grammar named as original contribution

### C-5 (Executive Summary, p6) — Partial-truths / missing integrative layer thesis
> "Its central claim is that mental disorder cannot be adequately understood through any single lens: not solely as brain disease, chemical imbalance, cognitive distortion, trauma response, social stress, or diagnostic category. These are all partial truths. The missing integrative layer is the living process itself: the organism as a self-producing, structurally coupled, emotionally oriented, interoceptively felt, meaning-making unity." [anchor: §Executive Summary · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-living-coherence-and-neuropsychiatric-di · pdf-p6]

### C-6 (Executive Summary, p6) — First-principles derivation chain autopoiesis→structural coupling→valence→emotioning
> "A living system must conserve its organization despite continuous material turnover. This is the autopoietic requirement. Because no living system exists in isolation, it must remain viable through recurrent relation with a medium. This is structural coupling. Because not all perturbations are equal, living systems must be oriented toward what supports their viability and away from what threatens it. This is valence. In animals, valence becomes emotioning: bodily dispositions that open and close domains of possible action." [anchor: §Executive Summary · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-27-living-coherence-and-neuropsychiatric-di · pdf-p6]

> "Following Maturana, emotions are not merely private feelings. They are dynamic bodily dispositions that specify domains of possible action. ### §10.1 — Five steps to apply the grammar (verbatim)
> "First, stabilize acute risk and identify non-negotiable medical or safety concerns. Second, describe the present state across body, affect, cognition, relation, and meaning without prematurely reducing it to a diagnosis. Third, locate the dominant constraint and margin loss: what is blocked, and what reserve remains? Fourth, identify the current regulatory strategy, even when it is costly: avoidance, compulsion, dissociation, substance use, collapse, expansion, perfectionism, or relational control may all function as attempts to preserve viability. Fifth, name one small option that can be reopened safely. The aim is not to complete a checklist, but to locate where repair can begin without forcing the system beyond its margin." [anchor: §10.1 How to Use the Seven-Primitives Clinical Grammar · pdf-p17]

### Appendix A — First-Principles Derivation table (verbatim, p26)
10-step derivation (Step | Derivation | Psychiatric translation):
1. **Living unity** — "A living system must conserve itself as a unity despite material turnover." → "Symptoms must be read in relation to organismic self-maintenance and identity."
2. **Autopoiesis** — "The unity persists by producing components that regenerate the network that produces them." → "Severe illness disrupts self-maintenance: sleep, appetite, rhythm, repair, metabolism, cognition, relation."
3. **Medium** — "No living unity exists without relation to a medium." → "The patient is always body-in-world, not isolated brain or mind."
4. **Structural coupling** — "Recurrent perturbations produce a history of organism-medium congruence or mismatch." → "Trauma, attachment, school, work, culture, poverty, care, and exclusion become embodied histories."
5. **Valence** — "Perturbations matter as better or worse for viability." → "Mental suffering involves altered salience, threat, reward, shame, loss, or possibility."
6. **Emotioning** — "In animals, valence becomes bodily domains of possible action." → "Disorders can be seen as locked action-worlds: threat, collapse, ritual, expansion, dissociation."
7. **Felt sense** — "Bodily regulation becomes inwardly registered as the condition of this living body." → "Interoception, vitality, panic, dissociation, body image, and somatic distress become central."
