## Verbatim key claims (15 claims; selective-high-fidelity — substrate-grammar density warrants extended capture)

### C-1 (p6, Abstract) — Rupture-not-transition framing (LOAD-BEARING; substrate for entire viability-grammar cluster)
> "The global order has entered a rupture rather than a transition. Institutions, rules, and economic narratives that once coordinated stability now persist without delivering the outcomes they promise. This white paper offers a disciplined diagnosis of that rupture by identifying the core false assumptions — economic, monetary, political, and institutional — that continue to guide policy despite mounting evidence of their failure."

### C-2 (p6, Abstract) — Objective falsity definition (LOAD-BEARING; recurs across Feb cluster)
> "It argues that the persistence of a rules-based vocabulary without life-coherent outcomes constitutes a form of **objective falsity**: systems appear functional by internal metrics while undermining the conditions of their own reproduction."

### C-3 (p7, Foreword) — McMurtry epigraph + epistemic-coordination framing
> "As the Canadian philosopher John McMurtry observed, *knowledge always wins in the end — but not unless and until it is known*. Systems do not correct themselves simply because better explanations exist somewhere. They correct only when false premises are explicitly identified, named, and replaced with criteria that correspond to the conditions of life they must serve."

### C-4 (p19, §4) — Living within a lie as structural-not-psychological (LOAD-BEARING; Havel-grammar; resonates with Spore ADR-0048 substitution-trap)
> "The phrase 'living within a lie,' drawn from the Czech dissident Václav Havel, names a condition that is structural rather than psychological. It does not describe deliberate dishonesty, nor does it imply bad faith. It describes a situation in which individuals and institutions continue to enact claims they no longer believe to be fully true because those claims remain embedded in routines, incentives, and expectations."

### C-5 (p19, §4) — Failure of epistemic coordination as system failure-mode (LOAD-BEARING; resonates with Spore F6 failure-modes + ADR-0073 F1 sensor-oracle-governance)
> "Living within a lie, in this sense, is not a failure of character. It is a **failure of epistemic coordination**. When false premises are widely shared but rarely named, no single actor can easily abandon them without incurring disproportionate risk. The system becomes self-sealing: everyone waits for someone else to speak first."

### C-6 (p21, §5.1) — Monetary-scarcity lie (foundational; sibling to Wave-A money-exception essay)
> "The most foundational economic lie is the treatment of money as a scarce, naturally occurring resource analogous to gold, water, or labor. Public discourse routinely frames governments as financially constrained in the same way households are constrained, implying that essential investments must wait until 'funding is available.'
> 
> This claim is not merely misleading; it is structurally false in modern monetary systems. Sovereign governments that issue their own currency are not operationally constrained by revenue in order to spend. They are constrained by real resources, productive capacity, skills, energy availability, and ecological limits."

### C-7 (p22, §5.4) — Efficiency-erodes-resilience (LOAD-BEARING; canonical viability-grammar concept; sibling to Post #2 C-6 slack/buffers framing)
> "By stripping away buffers, diversity, and slack, efficiency-driven systems lose the capacity to absorb disturbance. Supply chains optimized for cost fail catastrophically under disruption. Healthcare systems run 'lean' collapse under surge. Energy grids without redundancy propagate failure rather than containing it.
> 
> The life-value consequence is that systems appear successful until the moment they fail — and then fail completely."

### C-8 (p24, §6.2) — Hollow sovereignty (resonates with Spore ADR-0042 structural-legitimacy)
> "Sovereignty is commonly defined in terms of legal recognition, territorial authority, and formal autonomy. While these elements matter, they are insufficient. A state that cannot feed its population, supply energy, maintain public health, or protect ecological preconditions is not sovereign in any meaningful sense, regardless of its legal status."

### C-9 (p32-33, §9) — Meta-lie "There is no alternative" (LOAD-BEARING; cluster-signature)
> "The insistence that 'there is no alternative' is therefore not an empirical statement. It is a political and epistemic move. It functions to foreclose debate precisely at the point where evidence of failure becomes undeniable.
> 
> The life-value consequence of this meta-lie is terminal. When societies accept that they cannot change systems that degrade the conditions of life, decline becomes self-fulfilling. Adaptation is reduced to coping. Reform is confined to the margins. Crises are managed, not resolved.
> 
> At this stage, knowledge no longer lacks content. It lacks permission."

### C-10 (p34, §10) — Life-value onto-axiology definition (LOAD-BEARING; foundational substrate)
> "Life-value onto-axiology begins with a deceptively simple question: **what must be preserved and expanded for any society, economy, or political order to remain viable over time?** Rather than starting with preferences, prices, or power, it begins with the **conditions of life itself** — those requirements without which no value of any kind can be realized."

### C-11 (p34, §10) — Coherence test as evaluative criterion (LOAD-BEARING)
> "Life-value onto-axiology provides a way to reverse that inversion without appealing to ideology or moral exhortation. It does so by introducing a **coherence test**:
> 
> A system is coherent if its operations reliably sustain and enhance the life capacities on which it depends.
> 
> A system is incoherent if it systematically degrades those capacities while claiming success by its own internal metrics."

### C-12 (p36, §11) — McMurtry objective-falsity definition (LOAD-BEARING; cluster-signature)
> "Objective falsity does not mean that a claim is subjectively wrong, unethical, or unpopular. It means that a system or proposition **contradicts the conditions required for its own continued operation**. In this sense, falsity is not a matter of opinion. It is a matter of coherence with reality."

### C-13 (p36-37, §11) — Three-condition objective-falsity test
> "A system is objectively false when:
> - it advances money-value while degrading life-value,
> - it claims success while undermining its own preconditions,
> - and it requires denial of its effects to continue functioning."

### C-14 (p45, §15.1) — Life coherence as governance criterion (LOAD-BEARING)
> "The defining feature of a life-coherent order is that **life capacity becomes the primary evaluative standard** against which policies, institutions, and economic arrangements are judged.
> 
> This does not eliminate other objectives — efficiency, innovation, security, growth — but it subordinates them to a higher-order test: do they protect and expand the real capacities of people and societies to live, adapt, and flourish across time?"

### C-15 (p45-46, §15.2) — Four life-capacity domains (LOAD-BEARING; reused across Feb cluster — sibling to Feb 7 white paper's "life-ground" definition)
> "A life-coherent order requires explicit recognition of the domains that constitute life capacity. These include, at minimum:
> - **Physical foundations**: clean air and water, nutritious food, shelter, energy reliability, and public health.
> - **Social foundations**: education, care systems, trust, inclusion, and the ability to participate meaningfully in social and economic life.
> - **Ecological foundations**: stable climate systems, biodiversity, soil and water regeneration, and limits on extraction consistent with renewal.
> - **Institutional foundations**: reliable public services, accountable governance, and the capacity to coordinate collective action under stress."
