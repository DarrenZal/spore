2. **"Crisis is one of allocation, not scarcity"** [pdf-p3] — verbatim from deck: "**Providing universal Decent Living Standards (DLS) for 8.5 billion people requires only a fraction of current global material and energy use. The crisis is one of allocation, not scarcity.**" DLS material requirement: 27.8 Gt (~30% of current use). DLS energy requirement: 125 EJ (vs current >400 EJ). Core analytic claim of Hickel's research synthesized: existing productive-capacity sufficient to eliminate deprivation WHILE remaining within planetary boundaries — the binding constraint is allocation-structure, not productive-capacity.

3. **Capitalist vs Ecosocialist Law of Value (4-row comparison)** [pdf-p4] — verbatim 4-axis comparison: Objective (Maximize Profit vs Fulfill Human Needs); Control (Undemocratic / Dictatorship of Capital vs Democratic determination of labor/resources); Production-emphasis (Overproduces SUVs, fast fashion, weapons, private jets vs Scales Down ecologically harmful luxury sectors); Underproduction-emphasis (Underproduces affordable housing, agroecology, clean transit vs Ensures universal basic services and decarbonization). Synthesized binary comparison of value-laws as governance-design choices.

4. **Core / Periphery World-System architecture** [pdf-p5] — verbatim: "**Capitalism requires a world economy strung between the Core (Global North) and the Periphery (Global South). 90% of global labor and resources exist in the South, but they are systematically organized to serve Core accumulation.**" Wallerstein-derived World-Systems-Analysis framing applied to Hickel's specific empirical case: forced supply of cheap labor + raw materials from Periphery; dependence on capital + producer goods imposed on Periphery.
