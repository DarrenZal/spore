## Verbatim key claims (10 claims; selective)

### C-1 (p4, Abstract) — Money-sequence vs Life-Value reframe
> "Drawing on John McMurtry's Life-Value Onto-Axiology and Johan Galtung's peace research, the framework proposes a Life-Value Metric that evaluates policies according to whether they expand or diminish the inclusive range of human thought, feeling, and action. Structural violence, war economies, and the erosion of public infrastructure are interpreted as measurable forms of systemic disvalue."

### C-2 (p4, Abstract — LOAD-BEARING) — AI as impartial policy auditor (DISTINCTIVE)
> "The paper further proposes the use of AI-assisted impartial auditing to evaluate policies according to life-value parameters and universal life necessities. A staged roadmap toward planetary solvency is outlined, emphasizing investment in the civil commons and regenerative systems capable of sustaining long-term human flourishing."

### C-3 (p5, Exec Summary — Core Problem) — Systemic incoherence definition
> "Modern economic systems operate largely according to what can be described as a money-sequence logic, in which financial capital becomes both the starting point and the final goal of economic activity. In such systems, the protection of ecosystems, public health, and social infrastructure is often treated as a cost rather than as the foundational purpose of the economy. This misalignment produces systemic incoherence: societies may appear economically successful while the life-support systems that sustain them — soil, water, public health systems, social cohesion, and ecological resilience — are steadily deteriorating."

### C-4 (p5, Exec Summary — Life-Value Metric — LOAD-BEARING) — Value/disvalue evaluative principle
> "Value: Policies and institutions that enable a more inclusive and sustainable range of human thought, feeling, and action.
> Disvalue: Policies and institutions that reduce, disable, or destroy the conditions necessary for life and human flourishing."

### C-5 (p5, Exec Summary — Civil Commons) — Civil Commons as social immune system
> "Within this framework, public institutions such as healthcare systems, education, clean water infrastructure, environmental protections, and legal safeguards constitute the civil commons — the social immune system that protects access to universal life necessities. Strengthening the civil commons increases societal resilience, improves long-term economic efficiency, and reduces the structural drivers of conflict and instability."

### C-6 (p5, Exec Summary — Three forms of violence — LOAD-BEARING) — Galtung-canonical triangle
> "Drawing on Johan Galtung's work in peace research, three reinforcing forms of violence are identified:
> • Structural violence: systems that prevent populations from meeting basic needs
> • Direct violence: physical destruction of life and infrastructure
> • Cultural violence: narratives and ideologies that normalize or justify harm
> Psychological dynamics such as chosenness myths, moral dichotomies, and repression-projection mechanisms can further reinforce these patterns, making destructive policies appear legitimate or necessary."

### C-7 (p6, Exec Summary — Roadmap — LOAD-BEARING) — Five-stage Planetary Solvency transition (DISTINCTIVE substrate primitive)
> "The framework outlines a staged pathway toward what it calls planetary solvency — a condition in which social and economic systems consistently generate more life-supporting capacity than they consume. This transition involves five stages:
> 1. Homeostasis: Stabilizing life-support systems and halting destructive practices.
> 2. Allostasis: Adaptive recovery through policies prioritizing life necessities.
> 3. Growth and Development: Expanding education, healthcare, and social capability.
> 4. Sustainability: Establishing regenerative relationships with ecosystems.
> 5. Planetary Solvency: Achieving a global system that produces net life-capital."

### C-8 (p9, §2.1 Primary Axiom — LOAD-BEARING) — Value/Disvalue binary axiom
> "The foundation of our new logic is the Primary Axiom of Value. It provides a clear binary that allows administrators to distinguish between productive growth and parasitic depletion:
> • Value: That which consists in or enables a more coherently inclusive range of thought, feeling, and action. Any policy that increases the capacity of the population to think, create, and live securely is, by definition, a value.
> • Disvalue: That which reduces, disables, or destroys any range of life-expression."

### C-9 (p11, §3.1 Money-Sequence Trap) — Decoupling-effect
> "When the 'money-capital' sequence becomes the sole priority, the system treats the protection of the environment or the well-being of workers as 'externalities' or 'costs' to be minimized, rather than the very purpose of the economy."

### C-10 (p13, §4.1 CMT Syndrome) — CMT/DMA syndromes (LOAD-BEARING; composes with Wave-4 #8 Invisible Architecture)
> "The CMT Syndrome (Chosenness-Myths-Trauma): Groups often believe they are 'Chosen' (by God or History) to lead or save the world. This belief is supported by 'Myths' of a glorious past and fueled by 'Traumas' — unhealed historical wounds that are used to justify present aggression as a permanent state of self-defense."
