### Capture Modes taxonomy (Ch 9 + Appendix A Glossary — SEVEN modes total)

Chapter 9 names four in body prose (Measurement Violence, Metric Capture, Implementation Violence, Commercial Capture); the Appendix A glossary expands to **seven** capture modes:
1. **Measurement Violence** — "The reduction of living reality to what is easily counted, such that what matters but remains unmeasured becomes ignored, devalued, or shifted onto patients, families, clinicians, communities, or ecosystems."
2. **Metric Capture** — "The process by which institutions optimize indicators rather than the life-capacity the indicators were meant to serve."
3. **Implementation Violence** — "Harm created when technically sound interventions are imposed in ways that ignore context, increase burden, reduce dignity, or fail to ask whether people can live with them."
4. **Commercial Capture** — "A pattern in which profit-driven systems shape disease-producing environments while framing the consequences as individual failure."
5. **Epistemic Capture** — "The narrowing of what counts as legitimate knowledge so that lived experience, emerging patterns, local knowledge, and uncertain suffering are prematurely dismissed."
6. **Algorithmic Capture** — "The use of digital systems, predictive models, or AI in ways that obscure responsibility, amplify bias, reduce persons to data profiles, or organize care around the tool rather than life."
7. **Commons Enclosure** — "The privatization, degradation, commodification, or restriction of shared life-support systems such as water, food, care, education, public knowledge, ecological stability, and public space."

### Six-Level Evidence Ladder (Appendix E, p318 — verbatim, with usage rules)

1. **Level 1 — Established Clinical Practice** — "Use with strongest authority. Do not displace with the life-coherent framework."
2. **Level 2 — Strong Mechanisms** — "Integrate confidently, while respecting disease and context specificity."
3. **Level 3 — Plausible Integrative Synthesis** — "Use as an organizing frame and hypothesis generator."
4. **Level 4 — Clinical Formulation Grammar** — "Use pragmatically; evaluate by clinical usefulness, safety, feasibility, dignity, and outcomes."
5. **Level 5 — Emerging or Contested Claims** — "Use calibrated openness. Avoid both dismissal and premature certainty."
6. **Level 6 — Metaphor and Heuristic** — "Use to clarify meaning and orientation. Do not treat as mechanism or proof."

### Minimum Sufficient Force — 8-step Decision Sequence (Appendix D, p317 — verbatim)

1. **Identify danger.** What harm may occur if action is delayed?
2. **Assess phase.** Is the person in acute danger, stabilization, repair, reintegration, palliation, or chronic maintenance?
3. **Assess margin.** How much complexity can the person carry now?
4. **Choose force.** What action is strong enough to protect life?
5. **Assess treatment burden.** What cost, side effect, fear, complexity, or disruption does the action create?
6. **Sequence care.** What must happen first, second, later?
7. **Monitor response.** What will show that the action is helping?
8. **Revise.** Should care be intensified, simplified, stopped, or redirected?
[anchor: §Appendix D Minimum Sufficient Force Table · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-28-life-coherent-medicine-healing-the-organ · pdf-p317]

### Exposure / Repair / Margins triad (Exec Summary §5, p8 + Glossary, p304-305 — verbatim)

- **Exposure** — "Any repeated burden acting on the organism-person, including physical, chemical, microbial, nutritional, psychosocial, commercial, digital, institutional, and ecological burdens."
- **Repair** — "The biological, relational, psychological, social, ecological, and institutional processes that restore integrity, regulation, function, dignity, and participation."
- **Restorative Margins** — "The reserves that allow persons, families, institutions, and ecosystems to absorb disturbance without collapse: time, energy, money, safety, trust, attention, biological reserve, care continuity, social support, and ecological stability."
[anchor: §Appendix A Glossary · Exposure/Repair/Restorative Margins · orn:source:bsahely-2026-05-28-life-coherent-medicine-healing-the-organ · pdf-p304-305]

### Five systems-level reframes (Exec Summary §8, p10 + glossary)

## Safeguards — "What Life-Coherent Medicine Is Not" (Exec Summary §10, p11 — verbatim)

> "Life-coherent medicine is not anti-biomedical, anti-medication, anti-psychiatry, anti-measurement, or anti-specialist care. It is not a single-cause theory. It is not exposure reductionism. It is not patient blame. It is not vague holism. It is not a promise that all illness can be healed. It is not a burden placed on clinicians alone."
