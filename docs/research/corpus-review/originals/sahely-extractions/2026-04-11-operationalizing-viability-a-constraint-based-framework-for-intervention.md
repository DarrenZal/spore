## Key concepts and structural moves

- **Four-component viability balance**: Viability defined as ability to sustain coherent trajectories over time. Depends on balance among **four interacting components**: **Load** (cumulative stressor), **Adaptation** (active responses), **Reserve** (capacity that has not been spent), **Structure** (alignment of system components). This 4-component frame is the operational simplification of #5's 7-primitive grammar — when reduced to constraint-based language, the seven primitives compress into four directly operable quantities.
- **The Load Trap (central failure mechanism)**: When load increases, the system mobilizes more adaptive response; this consumes reserve; with depleted reserve, structural integrity declines; declining structure increases vulnerability; increased vulnerability raises effective load; this triggers more adaptive response — *positive feedback loop where increasing effort depletes capacity and accelerates collapse*. **The "Trying Harder" Effect**: standard problem-solving intuition (when system stressed, push harder) is exactly the wrong move under load-trap dynamics.
- **Generality of Load Trap across domains**: Same pattern in clinical (cardiac decompensation / sepsis / acute renal failure under high diuretic load) / engineering (chemical plant under shock loading + over-control destabilizes) / economic (fiscal stimulus into supply-constrained inflation amplifies the spiral) / governance (escalating regulation under declining institutional trust). Cross-domain isomorphism.
- **From states to constraints (paradigm reframe)**: Traditional control thinks in terms of states (variables to be maintained within targets). Constraint-based navigation thinks in terms of constraints (boundaries within which trajectories must remain viable). Different mathematical object; different intervention discipline.
- **Direction rather than value**: The Viability Invariant tells you direction-of-change required, not target-value to reach. This is a navigation primitive, not a control primitive.
- **Dual-scale intervention paradigm**: 
  - **Acute instability**: direct control is necessary to restore minimal function (e.g. clinical resuscitation)
  - **Stabilized phase**: continued control increases strain; intervention must shift to constraint-based navigation (reduce load / restore reserve / limit excessive effort / correct structural misalignment)
  
  The dual-scale rule is the operational discipline that resolves the "when to push, when to release" tension that pure-control or pure-navigation approaches cannot.
- **Viability Navigation Protocol (4-step structured method)**: 
  1. Relational assessment of system state across L/A/R/S components
  2. Identify trajectory bounds (constraint-set in which viable trajectories live)
  3. Apply intervention guided by Direction (not Value)
  4. Iterate based on system response (closed-loop continuous adjustment)
- **Non-associativity and path-dependence**: When interventions are non-associative, order matters: applying intervention A before B produces different outcome than B before A. This is structural, not procedural — encoded in the geometry of the constraint-space.
