## Key concepts and structural moves

- **Systems as Fields, not Objects**: Traditional bounded-system models fail in distributed multi-agent contexts. Systems exist as fields of agents with partial visibility, local incentives, independent action. Coherence is not inherent; it must emerge through integration.
- **Distortion is structural (not anomaly)**: Distortion arises from distributed perception + signal filtering + incentive pressures. It is an intrinsic feature of multi-agent systems. Extends #1's "distortion-as-structural" claim from one-system to multi-agent-field layer.
- **Early-warning-as-relational**: Failure begins as divergence across agents + delays in response + fragmentation of interpretation + interference between actions. Captured via the **collective altimeter** (extension of #1's single-system altimeter).
- **Failure as ecology, not event**: Distributed process involving cascading interactions, feedback amplification, erosion of coordination. Collapse is endpoint, not beginning. Pre-figures Spore's F6 ecology-of-failure framing.
- **Coherence as relational and integrative**: Aligned perception + compatible action + trust (coupling) + preserved margin + ability to integrate across agents. **Coherence does not reside in parts** — it emerges from inter-agent integration. Each agent holds a local truth; the system depends on whether these can be integrated.
- **Integration as central problem**: Failure occurs when integration is obstructed (the **obstruction concept** from sheaf theory — when local sections cannot glue into a global section, the obstruction is what is informative).
- **Field-aware action / collective Wu-Wei**: Actions interact, signals propagate, effects amplify. Effective action in field-context is relational + minimally sufficient + non-escalatory. Wu-Wei at multi-agent scale.
- **Design enables (but cannot force) coherence**: Coherence cannot be forced. It can be supported through signal integrity + incentive alignment + transparency + margin preservation. The four design levers.
- **Intervention must be minimal at scale**: Large systems amplify intervention. Effective intervention calibrates conditions, acts through signals, preserves optionality, avoids overcorrection.
- **Coherence as continuous practice**: Coherence is not a static state; it is continuously maintained through shared perception + disciplined action + sustained trust across interacting agents.
