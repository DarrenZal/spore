## Verbatim key claims (~14 claims from pages 1-14)

### C-1 (p9, Abstract) — Minimal structural thesis (LOAD-BEARING)
> "This work advances a minimal structural thesis: living systems are bounded, history-sensitive dynamical systems whose continued existence depends on preserving connected viable futures under finite constraint. Drawing on dynamical systems theory, stress physiology, ecology, economics, and governance theory, the book formalizes viability topology as the minimal metaphysical structure underlying living systems."

### C-2 (p9, Abstract) — Topological formalism (CANONICAL)
> "Persistence requires sustained interiority within a viability set *K*. Repeated imbalance encodes structural deformation, producing curvature, tipping points, hysteresis, and basin multiplicity. Reachable future space 𝒱(s) defines optionality. Control processes function to preserve or expand this space. Ontology, epistemology, and axiology are therefore dynamically unified: boundary defines being, constraint forecasting defines knowledge, and preservation of viable futures defines minimal value."

### C-3 (p9, Abstract) — Central evaluative question (LOAD-BEARING)
> "The central evaluative question becomes: does a given action widen or narrow the space in which the future remains possible?"

### C-4 (p10, Exec Summary) — Six core claims (CANONICAL CORE)
> "The core claims are:
> 1. Every living system operates within a bounded viability set *K*.
> 2. Persistence requires remaining inside this boundary under finite constraint.
> 3. Repeated imbalance encodes structural deformation (entrenchment).
> 4. Deformation reshapes topology, producing tipping points and hysteresis.
> 5. Reachable future space 𝒱(s) defines optionality.
> 6. Value corresponds to preserving or expanding connected viable futures."

### C-5 (p10, Exec Summary) — Health/disease/economics/governance under framework
> "Under this framework: Health is sustained interiority with sufficient margin and corridor width. Chronic disease is basin narrowing under accumulated deformation. Economic instability emerges from curvature steepened by debt, inequality, and volatility. Governance functions as cross-scale control architecture preserving global viability."

### C-6 (p10, Exec Summary) — Triadic unification (LOAD-BEARING)
> "The unification extends to philosophy itself. Ontology, epistemology, and axiology are not separate disciplines but dynamically coupled phases of viability preservation: Being is bounded persistence. Knowing is constraint-sensitive forecasting. Value is preservation of connected futures."

### C-7 (p10, Exec Summary) — Non-ideological framing
> "The framework does not prescribe ideology. It provides structural criteria. A policy, institution, or intervention is viability-aligned if it widens connected viable space across temporal and cross-scale horizons."

### C-8 (p10, Exec Summary) — Central maxim (LOAD-BEARING)
> "**Preserve the space in which the future remains possible.**"

### C-9 (p11, Intro) — Central claim (LOAD-BEARING)
> "The central claim of this work is modest in scope yet radical in implication: Living systems share a minimal structural form — a bounded, history-sensitive topology within which viable futures must be preserved. This form, which we call **viability topology**, is not a metaphor. It is not an analogy borrowed from mathematics for rhetorical effect. It is the minimal structure required for any system that must maintain itself under constraint."

### C-10 (p11, §I) — Viability set definition
> "A living system is not defined primarily by its material composition but by its ability to remain within a region of state space that permits continued operation. This region — the viability set — separates states compatible with persistence from those that terminate it. To exist, such a system must remain inside this boundary. Persistence therefore depends on margin: the slack between current conditions and the boundary of collapse. When margin narrows, fragility increases. When margin disappears, tipping becomes inevitable."

### C-11 (p12, §I) — Memory-as-deformation (LOAD-BEARING; novel formulation)
> "Repeated imbalance — when demand exceeds replenishment — encodes structural deformation. This deformation alters the topology of the viable region itself. Basins narrow. Curvature steepens. Tipping points emerge. Recovery becomes more difficult than decline. Memory, in this framework, is **not recollection. It is deformation**."

### C-12 (p12, §I) — Unification across phenomena
> "From these simple premises, phenomena typically treated as distinct — stress, burnout, chronic disease, institutional rigidity, economic fragility — become expressions of a single structural process."

### C-13 (p12, §II) — Three classical philosophical domains collapsed
> "Within viability topology, [Ontology / Epistemology / Axiology] are not independent inquiries. They are phases of a single dynamical process. Ontology concerns the existence of the viability boundary. Epistemology concerns forecasting constraint to remain within it. Axiology concerns action that preserves reachable futures. The three domains collapse into structural unity. Being is sustained interiority under constraint. Knowing is constraint-sensitive modeling. Value is preservation of connected viable space."

### C-14 (p14, §VI) — Four-element grammar (HIGHEST-LOAD-BEARING for Spore-grammar comparison)
> "The grammar is simple. Constraint defines the field. Memory deforms it. Control navigates it. Value preserves it. The future exists only inside that structure."
