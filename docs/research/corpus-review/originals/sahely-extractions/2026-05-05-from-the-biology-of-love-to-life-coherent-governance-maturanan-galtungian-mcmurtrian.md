## 9-fact hand-curated SECONDARY-anchor extraction

1. **Central thesis: politics as conservation of coexistence, not management by external control** [pdf-p3 abstract] — verbatim: "Maturana's biology of love provides a biological-ethical foundation for rethinking politics as the **conservation of coexistence** rather than the management of populations by external control. For Maturana, love is not sentimentality but the relational domain in which the other arises as legitimate in coexistence." Integrates Maturana + Galtung + McMurtry + Ostrom into single framework. Politics becomes life-coherent "when institutions conserve and expand the conditions under which persons, communities, species, ecosystems, and future generations can live, develop, participate, repair, and coexist without domination."

2. **Maturana biology-of-love as social-biological ground** [pdf-p7-8 §2] — verbatim: "Love is the relational domain in which another living being appears as **legitimate in coexistence**. Without such legitimacy, social life becomes organized through fear, control, exclusion, obedience, and domination." Cites Maturana 2002. Political formulation: "A society organized against love is not merely morally deficient; it is **biologically incoherent** because it conserves social relations that narrow the possible actions of its members." Refuses sentimental reading — biology grounds the political claim.

3. **Violence triangle paired with life-coherence triangle (Galtung extension)** [pdf-p18-19 §8] — verbatim 3×3 table:
   - **Direct violence** ↔ **Direct care** (immediate injury / immediate protection, healing, recognition, repair)
   - **Structural violence** ↔ **Structural justice** (institutional deprivation / institutions securing universal life necessities)
   - **Cultural violence** ↔ **Cultural legitimacy** (symbolic legitimation of life-disabling arrangements / narratives, symbols, education, law, science that affirm dignity, reciprocity, ecological responsibility)
   - Final formulation: "**Peace is not merely the absence of violence; it is the organized presence of life-enabling conditions.**" Cites Galtung 1969 (Violence, Peace, and Peace Research) + Galtung 1990 (Cultural Violence). **Connects to Wave-7 anchor #6 Grammar of Violence**: this paper translates Wave-7's Galtung-McMurtry violence-substrate into positive life-coherence-substrate. Cross-cluster bridge.

4. **McMurtry life-value as criterion of the good** [pdf-p11-12 §4] — verbatim: "**The good is not what maximizes price, profit, growth, or preference satisfaction; the good is what enables a more coherently inclusive range of life-capacity.**" Cites McMurtry 2011. "The decisive shift is from money-value to life-value. Money-value measures what can be priced, exchanged, accumulated, or converted into return. Life-value asks whether a system enables or disables the range of thought, felt being, and action of living beings." Universal life necessities: breathable air, clean water, food, shelter, care, education, health protection, meaningful participation, ecological stability, freedom from violence, symbolic worlds that do not degrade legitimacy. "These are not subjective luxuries. They are conditions of life-capacity itself."

5. **Money-sequencing produces institutional pathology (costly conserved drift at social scale)** [pdf-p13-14 §5] — verbatim: "**Institutional pathology arises when organizations conserve financial viability by disabling biological, social, ecological, or intergenerational viability.**" McMurtry-derived; framed as "the political analogue of costly conserved drift" (linking to Anchor #4's six-stage chronic-disease grammar). Examples: hospitals conserve billing while patients lose care; schools conserve metrics while children lose curiosity; food systems conserve profit while bodies lose metabolic health; water systems conserve extraction while watersheds lose viability; media conserve attention capture while public reason disintegrates; political systems conserve donors and parties while civil commons decay. Cites McMurtry 2011 critique of corporate-rights globalization.

6. **Civil commons as social metabolism of living coherence** [pdf-p15-17 §6] — Civil commons (McMurtry) reframed as "the social-institutional infrastructures that conserve and enable living coherence across persons, communities, species, ecosystems, and generations." Healthcare-as-civil-commons, education-as-civil-commons, water-as-civil-commons, knowledge-as-civil-commons all named explicitly. Distinct from market (price-signaled) and command (state-directed) — third governance mode. Integrates Ostrom (1990) commons governance (§7) into this layer: durable governance "depends on participation, contextual fit, monitoring, feedback, and collective rule-making rather than abstract command." **Connects structurally to Spore's commitment-pool / federation-encounter pattern (ADR-0068)**: civil-commons-as-meeting-substrate parallels federation-encounter-as-multi-actor-coordination-substrate. Descriptive only.

7. **Rights require civil-commons grounding to be socially real** [pdf-p20 §9] — verbatim: "**A right is socially real only when the civil commons required to exercise it is protected.**" McMurtry-derived. Right to health requires water+food+sanitation+care+ecological safety+housing+education+peace. Right to education requires schools+teachers+safety+language+time+public trust+digital access. Right to participation requires literacy+non-coercion+public space+time+institutional responsiveness. Shifts politics "from abstract entitlement to institutional responsibility." Critique of rights doctrines that float free of life-grounding.

8. **Life-coherent governance: 10-point diagnostic dashboard** [pdf-p22 §11] — Governance evaluated by whether it:
   1. protects universal life necessities;
   2. reduces direct, structural, and cultural violence;
   3. expands coherent ranges of thought, felt being, and action;
   4. protects ecological life-support systems;
   5. enables democratic participation and mutual recognition;
   6. prevents institutional capture by money-sequencing;
   7. strengthens civil commons;
   8. supports local self-organization with accountability;
   9. protects future generations;
   10. repairs harm without reproducing domination.
   Synthesizes Maturana legitimacy-of-the-other + Galtung violence-analysis + McMurtry life-value-criterion + Ostrom commons-governance. Core operational question: "**Does this institution conserve and expand life-capacity, or does it conserve itself by disabling life?**"

9. **Non-forcing politics as wu-wei governance** [pdf-p24-25 §12] — verbatim: "The political equivalent of wu-wei is: **Act so that persons, communities, and ecosystems regain their own capacity for viable self-organization, repair, and co-drift.**" Maturana's structural-determination (living systems cannot be instructed from outside like machines) + Ostrom's commons-work (durable governance depends on participation+contextual fit+monitoring+feedback+collective rule-making) + McMurtry's life-value criterion (self-organization remains accountable to life-needs, not captured by local domination or market power). "Non-forcing governance asks: 1. What life-capacity is being disabled? 2. What direct, structural, or cultural violence is being conserved? 3. What civil commons is missing, degraded, or captured? 4. What local capacities for self-organization already exist? 5. ..." **Parallels Anchor #4's non-forcing-action doctrine across 5 domains**; here applied to governance specifically.
