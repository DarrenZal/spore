## Verbatim key claims (8 claims; broader-cluster depth)

### C-1 (p5, Abstract) — Central thesis
> "This work offers a contemplative synthesis of the Four Gospels through integral–nondual and mythopoetic lenses. Rather than treating Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John as parallel historical accounts or doctrinal sources alone, it approaches them as four complementary perspectives revealing a single movement: from human experiences of separation toward communion with God, self, others, and reality itself."

### C-2 (p5, Abstract) — Embodied, not escapist
> "The work argues that the Gospel vision is neither escapist nor abstract, but embodied, relational, and oriented toward lived communion in a fractured world."

### C-3 (p18, §4 opening) — Perceptual-distortion diagnosis
> "Beneath the diversity of cultural forms, moral failures, and historical circumstances lies a shared predicament: the experience of separation. This separation is lived in multiple registers — psychological, social, spiritual — and yet it expresses a single underlying distortion in how reality is perceived and inhabited."

### C-4 (p18, §4 Experience of Separation) — Unity-as-unrecognized claim (composes weakly with Spore Holon-irreducibility framing per ADR-0050; theology layer, not primitive layer)
> "From an integral–nondual perspective, this condition arises not because unity is absent, but because it is unrecognized. The belief in separation — between self and God, self and others, self and the world — functions as a kind of perceptual distortion. Life is experienced as fragmented because it is lived from a fragmented sense of identity."

### C-5 (p19, §4 Failure of Substitutes) — Diagnostic move (NOT moralistic)
> "This inversion is not moralistic; it is diagnostic. It reveals that the obstacle to union is not imperfection, but self-enclosure."

### C-6 (p19, §4 Path of Transformation) — Four-phase pattern
> "Against this backdrop, the Gospels articulate a path that is neither escape nor domination, but transformation. This path unfolds through a pattern repeated across the narratives:
> • recognition of lack or blindness,
> • encounter that disrupts habitual identity,
> • surrender of false securities,
> • and emergence into a wider life."

### C-7 (p20, §4 From Individual Healing to Communal Renewal) — Structural-not-individual claim (parallels Sahely's structural-violence framing in 2026-02-24 papers)
> "This communal dimension reveals the full scope of the human predicament. Separation is not only a personal illusion; it is embedded in structures, habits, and institutions. Healing therefore requires not only individual awakening but collective reorientation."

### C-8 (p22, §5 Living the Pattern) — Orientation-not-prescription
> "Ultimately, the Gospels invite a way of living shaped by a recognizable pattern:
> • attention rather than distraction,
> • openness rather than control,
> • trust rather than fear,
> • participation rather than separation.
> This pattern is not imposed uniformly. It takes different forms in different lives and contexts. What unites these forms is orientation rather than outcome — a steady turning toward communion, even amid uncertainty and loss."
