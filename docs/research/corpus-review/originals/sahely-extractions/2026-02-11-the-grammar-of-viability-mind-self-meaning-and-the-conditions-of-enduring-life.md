## Verbatim key claims (14 claims; high-fidelity)

### C-1 (p3, Abstract) — Viability as unifying invariant (LOAD-BEARING)
> "This work proposes **viability** as a unifying invariant across living systems: the capacity to remain within the constraints that keep futures open."

### C-2 (p3, Abstract) — Multi-disciplinary substrate sources
> "Building on systems theory, affective neuroscience, depth psychology, philosophy of mind, and ancient wisdom traditions, the book develops a coherent grammar linking constraint, qualia, selfhood, memory, culture, and ethics. It argues that feeling is the first-person sensing of viability limits; the self is an interface managing continuity across time; recurring patterns emerge as memory without a single address; and ethics arises wherever viability must be negotiated among others and across generations."

### C-3 (p3, Abstract) — Constraint-faithful lens
> "Neither reductionist nor mystical, *The Grammar of Viability* reframes soul and spirit as functional realities, diagnoses institutional and cultural failure modes, and offers a disciplined framework for restoring orientation in an era of accelerating constraint. The work does not present a final theory, but a **constraint-faithful lens** capable of integrating knowledge without erasing difference — aimed at preserving coherence where it matters most."

### C-4 (p3-4, Executive Summary, claim 1) — Silent invariant (LOAD-BEARING)
> "**Viability is the silent invariant of living systems.** All living systems must remain within non-negotiable tolerance bounds to persist. Viability is not flourishing or optimization, but continued possibility across time."

### C-5 (p4, Executive Summary, claim 2) — Feeling exists to sense viability
> "**Feeling exists to sense viability from the inside.** Qualia and affect are not epiphenomena; they are compressed first-person signals that orient systems toward or away from viable trajectories."

### C-6 (p4, Executive Summary, claim 3) — Self as interface (LOAD-BEARING; consistent with Wave-4 #4)
> "**The self is an interface, not an essence or illusion.** Selfhood integrates feeling, memory, and anticipation to manage continuity across time. Identity is a necessary compression that becomes pathological when rigid."

### C-7 (p4, Executive Summary, claim 4) — Memory without a single address (LOAD-BEARING — composes with Spore Field rule-in-use vs rule-in-form per ADR-0046 / ADR-0041)
> "**Patterns persist as memory without a single address.** Recurring forms, skills, and archetypes arise from attractor landscapes shaped by repeated success under constraint, not from localized storage or mystical transmission."

### C-8 (p4, Executive Summary, claim 5) — Soul and spirit as functional realities
> "**Soul and spirit are functional realities.** Soul names lived continuity and personal coherence across time; spirit names sensitivity to trans-personal constraints such as ecological limits, trust, and intergenerational responsibility."

### C-9 (p4, Executive Summary, claim 6) — Ethics emerges where futures shared
> "**Ethics emerges wherever futures must be shared.** Ethics is not imposed from above but arises from the need to coordinate viability among multiple agents across time. Violations of ethics are violations of future possibility."

### C-10 (p4, Executive Summary, claim 7) — Collapse as delayed feedback (LOAD-BEARING — composes with Wave-4 #2 viability illiteracy + Wave-4 #6 cost of staying alive)
> "**Collapse is delayed feedback, not surprise.** When metrics, institutions, or ideologies lose contact with viability constraints, systems appear stable until correction becomes catastrophic."

### C-11 (p4, Executive Summary closing) — Orientation, not certainty
> "The book concludes that understanding itself can function as an adaptive capacity — if it remains disciplined by constraint, responsive to feedback, and integrated across scales. *The Grammar of Viability* offers not certainty or prediction, but orientation: a way to recognize what cannot be ignored, what must be integrated, and what it means to keep futures open."

### C-12 (p5, §Chapter 1 opening) — Knowledge-vs-orientation paradox
> "We live amid an abundance of knowledge and a shortage of orientation. Never before has a species known so much about the world and understood so little about how its pieces belong together. We can map genomes, image galaxies, manipulate markets, and model climates — yet we remain uncertain about something far more basic: **what kind of system we are living in, and what keeps it intact**. The problem is not ignorance. It is fragmentation."

### C-13 (p5, §Chapter 1 The success that hid the failure) — Reduction-without-error diagnosis
> "The fragmentation of knowledge did not arise from error. It arose from success. The scientific revolution learned how to make progress by narrowing its questions. It isolated variables, simplified systems, and bracketed everything that could not be controlled. This strategy worked extraordinarily well for studying inert matter and repeatable processes. It also created a habit of thought: **treat what cannot be isolated as secondary or subjective**."

### C-14 (p5, §Chapter 1 closing) — What was lost was constraint (LOAD-BEARING)
> "Life was reduced to chemistry. Mind was reduced to computation. Value was reduced to preference. Meaning was reduced to narrative. None of these reductions were wholly wrong. Each captured something real. But together they introduced a quiet distortion: **they severed explanation from the conditions that make systems endure**. What was lost was not spirituality, nor mystery, nor wonder. What was lost was *constraint*."
