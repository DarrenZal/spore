### C-1 (p5, Abstract — central thesis, LOAD-BEARING)
> "Orthodox economics presents itself as a science, yet it systematically fails to distinguish economic success from economic self-consumption. Gross domestic product, profit maximization, and monetary growth routinely rise alongside deteriorating public health, ecological depletion, institutional fragility, and declining life resilience. This paper argues that these failures are not empirical anomalies or regulatory lapses, but the predictable outcome of a foundational error: economics has optimized a symbolic proxy — money — rather than the life-support conditions it depends upon." `[pdf-p5]`

### C-3 (p5, Abstract — result framing)
> "The paper demonstrates why orthodox economics systematically misclassifies capital, underestimates systemic risk, and selects for life-capital depletion, and it provides a mathematically coherent diagnostic and therapeutic framework for banking, finance, regulation, and public policy. The result is not an ideological alternative to economics, but its scientific completion." `[pdf-p5]`

### C-5 (p6, Executive Summary §The Diagnosis — five failure modes, LOAD-BEARING for F6)
Orthodox economics fails five scientific-adequacy criteria:
> "**Incorrect state variables:** prices and money flows substitute for life-necessity sufficiency. **Misidentified capital:** monetary assets are conflated with regenerative capacity. **Invalid efficiency metrics:** profit and cost minimization replace ecological, physical, and human efficiency. **Proxy-based development measures:** GDP rises while life access declines. **Systemic risk blindness:** life-capital depletion is excluded from risk models." `[pdf-p6]`

### C-6 (p6-7, Executive Summary §The Solution — six reconstruction pillars)
> "1. **A Primary Axiom of Value** — Value is defined by the expansion of coherent life-range (thought, felt being, action); disvalue by its contraction. 2. **A Correct Economic State Space** — Universal human life necessities — air, water, food, shelter, healthcare, education, meaningful work, ecological integrity, and social inclusion — replace prices as the core variables. 3. **A Scientific Definition of Development** — Development is the expansion of secure access to life goods across the population and through time, not transaction volume. 4. **A Regenerative Concept of Capital** — Life capital is that which produces life goods without loss across generations; false capital yields returns by consuming these foundations. 5. **A Life-Coherent Definition of Efficiency** — Efficiency is achieved only when ecological, physical, and human development efficiencies improve together. 6. **Restoration of the Civil Commons** — Public health, water systems, education, legal institutions, and ecological protections are recognized as primary economic infrastructure." `[pdf-p6-7]`

### C-9 (p9, Part I §3 — The Proxy Error, canonical formulation)
> "Money is indispensable to modern economies — but it is not what economies are *about*. Money is a **symbolic coordination system**: a set of claims that facilitates exchange, accounting, and deferred obligation. It is a means. It is not a life requirement. It cannot be eaten, drunk, breathed, inhabited, or trusted. Its value depends entirely on the life-supporting infrastructures that stand behind it. Orthodox economics commits what can be called the **proxy error**: it treats money and price aggregates as if they were intrinsic measures of economic success rather than *derivative indicators* whose validity depends on external conditions." `[pdf-p9]`

### C-10 (p10, Part I §4 — GDP Paradox, canonical formulation)
> "Pollution clean-up, disaster reconstruction, rising healthcare expenditures due to preventable disease, and security costs associated with social breakdown all register as positive contributions to GDP. McMurtry explicitly rejects GDP as a measure of development unless it is made to correspond to access to life goods. From a scientific standpoint, this is decisive. A metric that can rise while the system it is supposed to measure deteriorates is **not a valid performance indicator**." `[pdf-p10]`

### C-11 (p11, Part I §5 — life-coherence constraint, LOAD-BEARING)
> "McMurtry's contribution is to identify the **life-coherence constraint** that economics has left implicit and therefore violated with impunity. An economy that undermines breathable air, potable water, ecological stability, public health, education, social inclusion, or meaningful work is not merely unjust. It is **malfunctioning as an economy**, because it is degrading the conditions of its own reproduction." `[pdf-p11]`

### C-12 (p12, Part II §4 — Primary Axiom of Value, LOAD-BEARING McMurtry verbatim)
> "**X is value if and only if, and to the extent that, it consists in or enables a more coherently inclusive range of thought, feeling, and action than without it.** Conversely, X is disvalue if and only if, and to the extent that, it reduces, disables, or destroys any such range." `[pdf-p12]`

### C-15 (p17, Part III §7 — Life-Coherence Economic State Model, FORMAL)
> "Formally, the economic state at time *t* can be represented as a vector of sufficiencies: x(t) = (s₁(t), s₂(t), … , sₙ(t)) where each sᵢ(t) measures the sufficiency of a specific life necessity — clean water access, nutritional adequacy, housing security, healthcare availability, educational access, meaningful work, ecological stability, and so on. This is not an abstraction imposed on the economy; it is the **economy's real condition**." `[pdf-p17]`

### C-16 (p18, Part III §8 — Life Capital definition, LOAD-BEARING)
> "He defines **Life Capital** as: **The wealth of means of life and life goods that produce more without loss in cumulative yield through time.** This definition introduces a **temporal regeneration criterion** absent from orthodox theory. Life Capital includes: ecological systems that renew soil, water, and biodiversity, public health systems that maintain population vitality, education systems that expand cognitive and adaptive capacity, social and institutional systems that sustain trust and cooperation, knowledge systems that compound understanding without depletion. What unites these is not ownership form but **regenerative function**." `[pdf-p18]`

### C-17 (p19, Part III §9 — Civil Commons as Core Economic Infrastructure, LOAD-BEARING for Spore-Ruddick bridge)
> "He refers to these as the **civil commons**: socially constructed systems that enable universal access to life goods and cannot function if subordinated to private profit extraction. These include: public water systems, sanitation and waste management, public health infrastructure, education systems, legal and institutional frameworks that secure rights and trust, environmental protections that preserve shared life conditions. Orthodox economics typically treats these as 'public expenditures' or 'externalities.' McMurtry shows that this framing is inverted. Markets and private enterprise **presuppose** the civil commons. They cannot exist without stable ecosystems, educated populations, healthy workers, enforceable contracts, and social trust. Yet orthodox models treat these enabling conditions as peripheral rather than foundational. In a life-coherent economics, the civil commons are recognized as **primary economic infrastructure** — the base layer upon which all market activity depends. Their erosion is therefore not a fiscal choice among others; it is a **direct contraction of life capital**." `[pdf-p19]`

### C-18 (p19-20, Part III §10 — Three Necessary Efficiencies)
> "**Ecological efficiency** — Inputs and processes must enable the provision of life goods with diminishing waste, pollution, and ecosystem damage. **Physical input–output efficiency** — Reduced material, energy, space, and mandatory labor inputs must produce the same or greater life-good outputs. **Human development efficiency** — Productive agents must be enabled to achieve greater life-range, life-time, and life-goods provision through education, health, and meaningful work. These efficiencies are not optional supplements to profit. They are **the only efficiencies that count** in a science of economy." `[pdf-p19-20]`

### C-19 (p20, Part III §11 — selection mechanism, LOAD-BEARING)
> "By defining capital in monetary terms, defining efficiency as profit, defining development as transaction volume, the system **selects for activities that convert life capital into money claims**. This is not a moral accusation; it is a selection mechanism. Firms, investors, and institutions that extract value fastest from life systems outperform those that regenerate them — until the underlying systems fail. McMurtry's framework reveals that what appears as market success can be **systemic self-consumption**." `[pdf-p20]`

### C-20 (p21, Part III §12 — completion-of-economics framing)
> "The economy's **state variables** are life-necessity sufficiencies. Its **capital stock** is life capital, not money. Its **infrastructure** is the civil commons. Its **efficiency criteria** are ecological, physical, and human development efficiencies. Its **objective** is the expansion of life-range across generations. … This is not an ideological replacement of economics. It is the **completion of economics as a science** by restoring its connection to the life conditions it must serve." `[pdf-p21]`

### §4.1 Money-Sequence-vs-Life-Sequence as substitution-trap at economic-civilization scale (deepens C1)

Sahely's selection-mechanism (C-19) articulates a structural shape that is operationally identical to W2.4's Golden Calf Trap at the economic-civilization layer: orthodox economics defines capital monetarily, efficiency as profit, development as transaction volume — and the resulting institutional logic **selects for activities that convert life capital into money claims**. Firms, investors, and institutions that extract value fastest outperform those that regenerate it, until the underlying systems fail. This is "not a moral accusation; it is a selection mechanism" (C-19).

This is the canonical paradigm-case substitution-trap shape at civilization-economic scale:

- The corrective distinction (money as proxy for life-supporting infrastructure) was originally introduced as a useful abstraction enabling exchange and deferred obligation (C-9: "Money is a symbolic coordination system... a set of claims that facilitates exchange, accounting, and deferred obligation. It is a means.").
- The corrective distinction was then captured + hardened + ritualized: GDP, profit, and monetary growth became treated as intrinsic measures of economic success rather than derivative indicators whose validity depends on external life-supporting conditions (C-9: "Orthodox economics commits what can be called the proxy error: it treats money and price aggregates as if they were intrinsic measures of economic success").
- The captured distinction now operates as **selection mechanism** systemically rewarding the very behavior the abstraction was supposed to facilitate (transparent exchange) at the cost of the underlying reality (life-supporting infrastructure).
- The GDP Paradox (C-10) demonstrates the captured-metric pathology operationally: "Pollution clean-up, disaster reconstruction, rising healthcare expenditures due to preventable disease, and security costs associated with social breakdown all register as positive contributions to GDP."

Under the C1 framing-note layer (per ratified Option D Bundle β), this maps cleanly onto Money-Value-as-false-invariant (W2.6 §4.7 paradigm-case): the economic-layer Golden Calf Trap is **the symbol (money) displacing the reality it was supposed to track (life-supporting conditions)**. This anchor provides standalone book-length treatment of what W2.6 §4.7 articulated as a single principle — making the economic-civilization-scale substitution-trap canonical-articulation-ready under C1 admission.

The composition is descriptive enrichment of an already-ratified framing-note layer; no new admission-pressure is surfaced.
