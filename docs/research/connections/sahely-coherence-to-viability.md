## 2. C-claims (verbatim from Phase 1 extraction; load-bearing claims; pdf-p anchors)

Anchors carry RID `orn:source:bsahely-2026-03-22-from-coherence-to-viability-a-geometry` and pdf-p<page>. Page-ranges per Phase 1 extraction `read_call_log`.

- **C-1** [anchor: Abstract · pdf-p11] Central thesis (LOAD-BEARING): *"Complex systems across domains — clinical, ecological, and economic — frequently fail despite the availability of extensive data, advanced analytics, and well-intentioned interventions. This work proposes that such failures arise not primarily from insufficient information or incorrect values, but from a loss of relational coherence within system structure."*

- **C-2** [anchor: Abstract · pdf-p11] 7 primitives + Fano plane (LOAD-BEARING; matches W1.5 #5 heptad; XR duplication corrected to XO via Ch 7): *"We introduce a minimal, domain-agnostic framework termed the Geometry of Viability, composed of seven primitives: State (X), Constraints (C), Margins (M), Disturbances (D), Perception (P), Regulation (R), and Options (O). These elements are not analyzed in isolation but through their structured relationships, organized into triads corresponding to a minimal closed system represented geometrically by the Fano plane."*

- **C-3** [anchor: Abstract · pdf-p11] Three-level invariant hierarchy (LOAD-BEARING; full mathematical-formalism hierarchy): *"The framework is further formalized through a hierarchy of invariants: pairwise compatibility (ω), triadic coherence (N3), and global viability (I4). Together, these define necessary conditions for system persistence across scales."*

- **C-4** [anchor: Abstract · pdf-p11] Mathematics-as-navigation reframe: *"A central contribution of this work is the reframing of mathematics from a predictive tool to a navigational framework, capable of mapping constraints on possible transitions rather than specifying future states. This shift supports a broader paradigm transition from control-oriented intervention to constraint-aware navigation."*

- **C-5** [anchor: Abstract · pdf-p11] Final unifying principle: *"Systems remain viable not by controlling outcomes, but by navigating the space of possibilities within constraints."*

- **C-7** [anchor: Exec Summary "The Proposal" · pdf-p12] Seven primitives with definitions: *"This work introduces a minimal grammar of viability, consisting of seven primitives: State (X) — current system condition; Constraints (C) — boundaries of viability; Margins (M) — capacity to absorb disturbance; Disturbances (D) — forces acting on the system; Perception (P) — interpretation of system and environment; Regulation (R) — actions taken in response; Options (O) — available pathways for adaptation. These elements are organized into seven triads, forming a closed relational structure mapped to the Fano plane."*

- **C-8** [anchor: Exec Summary "The Structure" · pdf-p13] Three-scale architecture (LOAD-BEARING): *"The framework operates across three levels: 1. Local (ω — Pairwise Compatibility) — Determines which interactions are structurally permissible. 2. Meso (N3 — Triadic Coherence) — Determines whether interactions form coherent relational units. 3. Global (I4 — System Viability) — Determines whether the system can persist as a whole. Failure at any level can propagate across the system."*

- **C-9** [anchor: Key Insight · pdf-p14] Stability = coherence-across-change (LOAD-BEARING; composes with Maturana conservation-through-change): *"Stability is not the absence of change, but the preservation of coherence across change."*

- **C-10** [anchor: Ch 17.1 · pdf-p72] Hierarchy as constraint-sequence (LOAD-BEARING): *"ω (Symplectic Pairings) — governing pairwise compatibility. N3 (Cubic Norm) — governing triadic coherence. I4 (Quartic Invariant) — governing global viability. These are not independent constructs. They form a hierarchical system of invariants, in which each level builds upon and constrains the next. [...] This hierarchy can be understood as a sequence of constraints: 1. ω filters pairs → defines what interactions are possible. 2. N3 filters triads → defines what groupings are coherent. 3. I4 filters configurations → defines what systems are viable. Each level is necessary but not sufficient for the next. A system may satisfy ω but fail N3; it may satisfy N3 locally but fail I4 globally."*

- **C-11** [anchor: Ch 17.2 · pdf-p73] Multi-scale coherence (LOAD-BEARING; sheaf-precursor framing): *"This multi-scale perspective aligns with observations across domains, where failures often occur not because of isolated breakdowns, but because of misalignment across scales."*

- **C-12** [anchor: Ch 17.3 · pdf-p74] Feasible region of navigation: *"To remain viable, a system must navigate in a way that preserves all three levels of coherence: Respecting ω (avoid interactions that are structurally incompatible). Maintaining N3 (ensure that its interactions form coherent triads). Preserving I4 (maintain global coherence). These constraints define a feasible region of navigation. Within this region, the system can move, adapt, and explore. Outside it, coherence is lost and viability is threatened."*
