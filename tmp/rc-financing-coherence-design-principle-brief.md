# Route capital through coherence, not preference — a design principle for the RC financing facility

*Internal working brief for the dedicated bioregional-financing session with Mehul, and for input into the concept note. Drawn from the 2026-05-31 Mehul 1:1 (§2) and Darren's concept-note comment on Output 3. **Not a send-ready artifact** — anything transmitted externally (or pasted into the funder-facing note) gets a final pass through the usual verification gate. Deliberately contains no mathematical vocabulary; this is the frontstage register.*

## The one line

**Capital should flow to *bundles that cohere*, not to projects ranked by appeal or similarity.** Fund the combination — ecological need + steward capacity + community legitimacy + evidence quality + regeneration potential — whose constraints actually hold together, rather than the single highest-scoring project.

## Why it matters here (the complement to the capability matrix)

The capability matrix scores **individual initiatives** across the five pathways — one initiative at a time. That's necessary but, on its own, it quietly pushes the instruments toward a **recommender / rank-by-similarity logic**: fund the top-scoring projects, or fund clusters of similar ones. The allocation logic the facility actually needs is **compositional**: the question isn't "which project scores highest?" but "which *combination* of partners and commitments forms a viable, fundable whole?"

So, as one line of design principle for **Output 3**:

> **Route capital through coherence, not only preference — fund the bundle whose ecological, social, and governance constraints actually hold together, rather than the single highest-scoring project.**

This is the move that keeps the instruments from defaulting to a recommender, and it's the natural complement to the matrix.

## Three principles, said out loud

1. **Coherence, not clustering.** A seed collector, a GIS mapper, an Indigenous governance steward, and a funder are **not similar** — and that's the point. They may *compose* a restoration commitment precisely because they differ in the right ways. Allocation should test **compatible differentiation**, not group-by-similarity. *Heterophily is the point.*

2. **Obstruction is information.** A bundle that *won't* close is not a failure to paper over. It tells you **where** and **why**: a missing bridge, an incompatible protocol, a circular dependency, an over-exposed data flow — or an irreducible difference that should be **protected, not optimised away**. And it points to **who can repair it**, without the facility pretending to own the legitimacy call.

3. **Don't cross the sovereignty boundary.** Some constraints (e.g. an Indigenous governance protocol) are not trade-offs to be balanced — they're boundaries the allocation must respect. Coherence means coordinating across difference, never flattening it into a forced agreement.

## This is buildable, not blue-sky

There's a **proven operational kernel** underneath: Will Ruddick / Grassroots Economics' work on **commitment-pool route graphs for finance and mutual aid** — formalised commitments, pooled into shared liquidity, with explicit settlement, limits, and repair. The facility's allocation layer is an extension of that, and the **existing BKC commitment pipeline** is the substrate to reuse — not something to rebuild.

The honest v0 is **ordinary, small, and auditable**: a routing diagnostic that, given a candidate set of initiatives, surfaces *which bundles compose, which don't, what's missing, and who can repair the gap.* Not a fancy dashboard — a defensible allocation aid.

## How to pitch it (and how not to)

- **Pitch:** *"This makes the facility's allocation logic legible and defensible to funders — and it's a fundable R&D thread in its own right."*
- **Not:** "let's spend the runway building allocation software." The **framing is usable in a financing-design conversation today** (Tier 1). **Building the diagnostic over real pools is a separate, fundable project** (Tier 2) — explicitly *not* a draw on RC's runway. Capital in the door comes first.

## A few things worth deciding together

- What's the **minimum useful unit** of a fundable bundle (which roles/commitments must be present for it to count as "composable")?
- How do we tell a **fixable** obstruction (a missing partner, a sequencing gap) from an **ethical / incommensurable** one that should be *preserved*?
- What should **"coherence" actually measure** in a bioregional pool — ecological outcome, governance legitimacy, trust, commitment fulfilment, or some named blend?
- Can we model it on **ValueFlows / REA / commitment-pooling** without importing market-dependence assumptions?
